## What EverydayNaira is

EverydayNaira is a practical money explainer brand. It is an educational publishing platform built to help everyday Nigerians understand and improve their everyday money decisions. The site covers budgeting, saving, banking, debt, income management, and money safety — the six areas where most people have real questions and where clearer information leads to better outcomes.

We write to reduce confusion, prevent avoidable mistakes, and give readers practical systems they can use in real life. Every article on this site is designed to help someone understand an issue and take a sensible next step — not to sound impressive or to sell them something.

> **Our core promise**
>
> We make money topics simpler, clearer, safer, and more useful for ordinary Nigerians. That is the standard every page on this site is held to.

## What EverydayNaira is not

EverydayNaira is not a licensed financial advisory service. We do not give personalised investment advice, legal guidance, or tax recommendations. We are a publisher and explainer — we explain, compare, and simplify, but the final decision on any financial matter always belongs to the reader.

We are also not a loan marketing platform, a crypto speculation site, a thin affiliate site, or an AI content farm. Every page on this site is built to be genuinely useful, properly sourced, and strong enough to publish on a trust-sensitive finance platform.

## Who we write for

Our primary readers are young Nigerians navigating everyday financial decisions — salary earners managing monthly spending, freelancers handling irregular income, students working with limited budgets, side hustlers mixing multiple income streams, and anyone trying to make better sense of how banking, debt, and savings actually work in Nigeria.

Many of our readers feel overwhelmed by money decisions, are not sure where to start, and distrust overly polished or fake-expert finance content. We write for that person. Our content is designed to be simple enough for a beginner and specific enough to be genuinely useful for someone already managing their money but trying to do it better.

## Our core values


### Clarity

We simplify money topics without dumbing them down. If a reader finishes an article more confused than when they started, we have not done our job.


### Honesty

We do not pretend to know what we do not know. We do not fake credentials, certainty, or expertise. When something is uncertain, we say so.


### Practicality

Every page must help readers do something useful — not just understand a definition. A good article leaves the reader clearer and better equipped to act.


### Trust

Credible sources, careful phrasing, and respect for the reader's real situation. We treat personal finance as the serious subject it is.


### Restraint

We do not overclaim, oversell, or exaggerate. Finance content that pressures, hypes, or misleads is exactly what we are building an alternative to.


### Humanity

Money is emotional for many readers. We write with empathy and without judgment — because struggling with money is a real experience, not a character flaw.

## How we approach content

Before any article is written, we confirm it solves a clear reader problem, fits our site structure, and can be supported with trustworthy sources. We use official Nigerian sources first — the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN), NIBSS, NDIC, SEC Nigeria, the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS), and official bank and fintech documentation. We use research institutions like EFInA and the World Bank for broader context.

We do not invent statistics, guess at fees, or treat rumour as fact. Where information is time-sensitive — charges, product terms, regulations — we frame it carefully and direct readers to official sources for current details. We update content when we become aware of material changes.

We prefer fewer, stronger pages over many weak ones. A page earns its place on this site by being genuinely useful — not by targeting a keyword or filling a content calendar.
