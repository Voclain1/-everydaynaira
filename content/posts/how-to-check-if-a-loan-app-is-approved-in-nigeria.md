A loan app promises ₦100,000 in five minutes. Its advertisement uses the words *approved*, *safe* and *instant*. Before installing it or sharing your contacts, how do you know whether the company is actually authorised to offer digital loans in Nigeria?

The safest starting point is **the lender's legal identity and the regulator's own current records**. An app-store rating is not a financial licence, and a borrowed company name in an advertisement proves little.

The [Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (FCCPC)](https://fccpc.gov.ng/registration-of-digital-money-lenders/approvals-of-dmls/) maintains a public list of digital lenders and their apps. Its published register is a key check, but **registration is not a recommendation that a particular loan is affordable or fair**.

## Step 1: identify the legal company behind the app

Check the lender's website, app-store developer information, privacy policy and written loan agreement. Write down:

- Legal company name, not just the app's trade name.
- Registered or official website and support contact.
- App name as shown in the relevant store.
- Address or corporate information provided in its terms.
- The company that actually disburses the money.

If an app says “powered by” a microfinance bank, identify the actual institution. Technology providers and lenders can be different companies. Do not assume the app-store publisher is the party responsible for the loan.

## Step 2: search the FCCPC's live lender register

Open the [official FCCPC approved-digital-lenders page](https://fccpc.gov.ng/registration-of-digital-money-lenders/approvals-of-dmls/), then search the **exact company name and app name**. Check the relevant approval category and date if displayed.

A loose match is not enough. Two businesses can have similar names, and an impersonator can copy a legitimate app's name or logo.

If the company isn't listed, do not conclude automatically that it is a scam: check whether it is operating through a different correctly licensed institution or whether the register has been updated. But **do not borrow until you can verify the accountable entity and its relevant authorisation**.

The [CBN publishes information on regulated financial institutions](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/supervision/allfininstitutions.asp) for institutions under its mandate. The right regulator depends on the legal entity and licence, not the app's marketing language.

## Step 3: understand the current 2026 rule

In 2025, the FCCPC issued the [Digital, Electronic, Online or Non-Traditional Consumer Lending Regulations](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/DIGITAL-CONSUMER-LENDING-2025-LATEST.pdf), often called the **DEON Regulations**.

The Commission temporarily suspended enforcement in spring 2026 because of a court order. On **20 July 2026**, it [announced that a Federal High Court decision had lifted the restraint and that implementation had resumed](https://fccpc.gov.ng/fccpc-resumes-digital-lending-regulation/).

This sequence matters: advice claiming the regulations are *still suspended* is outdated according to the FCCPC's subsequent July announcement. Regulatory enforcement and court appeals can evolve, so confirm the current status on the regulator's website when investigating a particular provider.

## Step 4: compare the loan's actual cost

An approved lender can still offer a loan that is wrong for your budget.

Before accepting, request the **full written loan offer** showing:

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| How much will enter your account? | Deductions can make net disbursement less than the approved amount |
| What is the total amount repayable? | This captures interest and stated fees |
| What are the repayment dates? | A short loan may require a large payment before your next income |
| What happens after a late payment? | Penalties and collection conduct matter |
| Are there early repayment charges? | They can change the cost of settling the debt |
| Which app permissions are requested? | Contact access and personal-data use deserve scrutiny |

A 3%-per-month flat loan may not have the same total cost as a 3% reducing-balance loan. See [our worked comparison](/flat-rate-vs-reducing-balance-loan-interest-in-nigeria/) before accepting a percentage at face value.

## Step 5: review privacy permissions

A lender may need identity and affordability information, but that does not justify every phone permission or harmful disclosure of your private information.

Read the privacy notice and assess requests for contacts, photos or files. If a service threatens to message relatives, publish your photograph or disclose your alleged debt, keep evidence and review [how to report loan-app harassment](/how-to-report-loan-app-harassment-in-nigeria/).

The [Nigeria Data Protection Commission](https://ndpc.gov.ng/) also has responsibility for personal-data protections.

## Red flags even when a company name looks familiar

- A download link directs you to an unofficial APK sent in a random chat.
- An agent requests your **OTP, bank password or complete card credentials**.
- The loan's fees or repayment date are disclosed only after acceptance.
- The company's name in the contract doesn't match the one you verified.
- The lender threatens public embarrassment as its collection strategy.
- You are asked to pay a mysterious upfront “release fee” to a personal account.

No single sign proves fraud, but several together should stop the process until you verify the lender independently.

## FAQs

### Is every loan app on Google Play approved by the FCCPC?

No. Store availability is not the same as the regulator's approval or registration status.

### Does FCCPC approval guarantee I'll be treated fairly?

No. The public register helps verify regulatory status; it does not make every offer suitable. Consumer complaints can still arise.

### Should I use a third-party list of approved apps?

Use the official FCCPC register as the source of record. Third-party lists can be helpful starting points but may be stale.

### What if a bank or CBN-licensed institution offers the loan?

Verify that institution and its applicable CBN licence and regulatory process. Do not assume all digital financial services have identical FCCPC registration requirements.

### What if the lender is abusive?

Preserve messages and the contract, make a complaint through verified channels and consider data-protection remedies. Read our [loan-app harassment complaint steps](/how-to-report-loan-app-harassment-in-nigeria/).

## The bottom line

The right test is **identity → register → full loan cost → data privacy → complaint route**. A legitimate-looking interface is not evidence; the regulator's current records and the actual contract are the starting point.
