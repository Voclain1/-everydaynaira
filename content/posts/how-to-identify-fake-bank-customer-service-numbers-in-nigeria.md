You search for your bank's support number. A sponsored result shows the correct logo and offers to resolve a failed transfer in minutes. The person you contact asks for a six-digit code.

**That may be bank-support impersonation.** A fake account can copy a logo, buy a search advert and respond faster than the bank. The [CBN's fraud-awareness page](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/supervision/cpdfraudandscam.html) warns about phishing: people presenting themselves as trusted institutions to extract secrets and money.

## Common ways fake support appears

Fraudulent channels may use a near-identical website address, a social-media account replying to a public complaint, a fake WhatsApp business profile or an SMS that appears in a message thread alongside legitimate bank alerts.

The account may know real banking phrases like NIP session ID, card restriction or chargeback. **Knowing these words proves nothing.**

## Verify the number through another trusted channel

If you have your bank's official app already installed, open its help or security section. Use the phone number, contact form or website shown there.

If your phone might have been compromised, use a different trusted device or visit a branch. When calling back, dial the number independently—do not simply press a link in the suspicious message.

For social media, follow the official bank website's verified link to the bank's account, rather than trusting a person who starts a new DM thread.

## A website padlock isn't a banking licence

HTTPS means the connection is encrypted. It does not mean the site belongs to the bank.

Look closely at the full domain, not just the brand name somewhere in it. Extra words, misplaced hyphens and misleading endings can make a fake site appear convincing.

Avoid unfamiliar APK downloads presented as customer-support updates. Such software can capture sensitive information or request inappropriate permissions.

## What genuine support should not ask you to do

| Suspicious instruction | Safer response |
| --- | --- |
| Give us the OTP to reverse the debit | Refuse; contact the bank independently |
| Enter your banking password on this link | Navigate from the official app or known website |
| Pay a fee into a personal account to unlock support | Ask the real institution to confirm any applicable charge |
| Install a remote-control app | Do not give a stranger access to your phone |
| Send the full card number, expiry and CVV | Stop and contact the bank's security channel |

Some genuine banking support interactions require identity checks. They should not require you to surrender secrets that authorise transactions.

## What if you already gave an OTP?

Contact your bank immediately using a new, verified channel. Ask it to secure affected cards, account access or transfer channels as appropriate.

Change exposed passwords from a trusted device and review recent account history. Preserve the suspicious conversation so the bank can investigate the incident.

If money has moved without your authorisation, use [the unauthorised-debit response guide](/what-to-do-after-an-unauthorized-debit-in-nigeria/). If you voluntarily sent a transfer to an impostor, use [our scam transfer guide](/what-to-do-after-sending-money-to-an-online-scammer-in-nigeria/).

## If you only clicked the link

Visiting a link does not automatically mean your account was accessed. Risk rises if you entered information, installed software or granted sensitive permissions.

Close the page, do not submit details, and review device security if you downloaded anything. Monitor for suspicious transactions and consider changing credentials if you think they were exposed.

## How to report the impostor

Save the profile URL, phone number, screenshot and date. Report the fake account to the social platform or search provider, and forward the relevant evidence to the bank's verified security team.

You can also report suspected fraud to appropriate official law-enforcement channels. Do not circulate the fraudulent link as a clickable “warning” that sends more people to it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a WhatsApp Business badge proof of bank ownership?

Not by itself. Confirm the contact from the bank's trusted website or app.

### Do banks ever call customers?

They may, but you can stop the conversation and contact the bank yourself. An unsolicited call is not a reason to reveal an OTP.

### Can scammers purchase Google advertisements?

Yes. Search placement alone does not verify the advertiser's identity.

### Does the bank need my transaction PIN to investigate?

You should not reveal your PIN, password or OTP to a support person. Use established secure verification processes.

### What if I told someone my account number?

An account number alone is different from giving a password or OTP. Still review the interaction and report anything suspicious.

## The one habit worth remembering

**Verify first, then explain your banking problem.** The faster person replying to a complaint is not necessarily the genuine bank agent.
