A bank keeps telling you the same thing: *Please wait for feedback.* You have waited, called again and been transferred to another department. Meanwhile your money, account access or complaint remains unresolved.

You do not have to keep starting from zero. There is a formal complaint route for customers of financial institutions regulated by the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN).

Here is how to document a complaint, contact the right institution and decide whether it is time to take the matter to the regulator. This article is about **complaint handling**, not an assurance that every disputed transaction must be refunded.

## Before you report a bank to the CBN

**Start with the institution that holds your account or processed the transaction.** The [CBN's official complaints-management guidance](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/supervision/cpdcomgt.html) says to report the issue to the relevant bank first, then allow the applicable time for it to investigate.

You may complain about:

- a failed transfer with no confirmed reversal;
- an unexplained debit or suspected unauthorised transaction;
- an incorrect or repeated charge;
- a dispute about your balance or transaction record;
- an unresolved request for information about a banking service.

Different cases can have different technical and complaint-resolution timelines. For a [failed transfer where you were debited](/bank-transfer-failed-but-debited-in-nigeria/), the underlying payment system may have a faster reversal expectation than the general timeline for escalating a complaint to CBN.

## Step 1: Lodge a formal complaint with your bank

Use a verified in-app help centre, the bank's official website or a branch. Describe only what you know: amount, date, transaction status and what went wrong.

Ask the bank to:

1. register your complaint;
2. give you a **complaint reference or tracking number**;
3. confirm the investigation and next update date; and
4. send its answer in writing, if possible.

A chat transcript that merely says “we are working on it” is less useful than a complaint number and a specific answer.

If there is a risk of ongoing fraud, tell the bank at once and request protective measures; do not wait two weeks to secure your account.

## Step 2: Prepare your evidence

Create one folder containing:

| Item | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Payment reference or session ID | Lets the bank locate the exact transaction |
| Date, time, amount and channel | Prevents a dispute about which event you mean |
| Screenshots or statement excerpt | Shows the actual debit, status or unexplained fee |
| First complaint date and tracking number | Establishes that you followed the correct process |
| Bank's response or lack of one | Shows what remains unresolved |
| Short statement of requested remedy | Makes the outcome you want unambiguous |

**Protect yourself:** CBN's guidance specifically says not to include banking passwords or PINs. Do not submit OTPs, full card-security information or login credentials.

## Step 3: Know when you can escalate

For ordinary complaints, the CBN's [published guide](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/FinInc/FinLit/LodgeComplaint.html) generally tells customers to allow **two weeks** after reporting the issue to their institution before escalating unresolved matters. Some products and payment problems have shorter or different operational timelines.

This is not a reason to delay reporting fraud or to ignore an urgent security issue. It is the usual route for *regulatory escalation of a complaint*. If the bank fails to register or acknowledge your complaint, seek clarification about the applicable procedure through the regulator's official channels.

Do not make up a complaint history or submit an escalation before you have contacted the institution.

## Step 4: Contact the CBN Consumer Protection Department

The [CBN complaints page](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/supervision/cpdcomgt.html) lists the Consumer Protection Department and provides an email address for escalation: **cpd@cbn.gov.ng**. You can also address a letter to the Director, Consumer Protection Department, CBN, Abuja, or submit one through a CBN office as directed.

There is also an official [CBN Consumer Complaints Management System portal](https://complaintsportal.cbn.gov.ng/). Follow the instructions currently shown on that website for registration, complaint references and tracking; portals change, so do not assume the screen will always look the same.

Contact only official domains. A random social account claiming to work for CBN does not become legitimate because it uses a logo.

## Copy-and-edit complaint letter

> **Subject: Complaint against [bank name] — unresolved [issue]**
>
> To the Consumer Protection Department, Central Bank of Nigeria:
>
> I am requesting assistance with an unresolved complaint against [bank name]. On [date] at [time], [describe the transaction or event]. The amount involved is ₦[amount], and the transaction reference is [reference].
>
> I first reported the matter to [bank name] on [date]. My complaint tracking number is [number]. The institution has [summarise its written response or failure to resolve the matter].
>
> I request [a verified reversal, corrected balance, explanation, fee refund or other specific remedy]. I attach a transaction receipt or relevant statement excerpt, my initial complaint and subsequent correspondence.
>
> Please confirm receipt and advise me on the next steps.
>
> [Your name and safe contact details]

Write facts, not insults. If a charge is merely unfamiliar, ask first whether it is allowed under the published [Guide to Charges](/bank-charges-in-nigeria-explained/).

## What happens after an escalation?

The regulator may seek information from the financial institution, examine the documents, ask follow-up questions or direct you to a different complaint channel if the provider is outside its scope.

There is no honest way to promise that CBN will order a refund in every case. Your evidence should help distinguish a proven service failure from an unresolved allegation or a legitimate charge.

Keep a single timeline of updates. When the bank contacts you with an offer or explanation, request that it be documented. Do not accept a stranger's request to withdraw your complaint in exchange for a fee.

## Common mistakes that weaken a complaint

- **No reference number:** you cannot easily show that the bank received the complaint.
- **Several separate stories:** your amount, dates and requested remedy should match throughout.
- **Only a screenshot of an SMS:** use the bank's transaction detail or statement where possible.
- **Incorrect category:** a [transfer to the wrong account](/how-to-reverse-money-sent-to-wrong-account-in-nigeria/) is not the same as a [failed transfer](/bank-transfer-failed-but-debited-in-nigeria/).
- **Sensitive credentials in an email:** no genuine complaint needs your OTP or password.
- **Submitting through imitation websites:** check the URL character by character.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I complain directly to the CBN without contacting my bank?

The CBN's standard published process asks you to report to the bank first. An immediate security incident still needs urgent bank action.

### Does reporting to CBN cost money?

Do not pay a private person to file a bank complaint for you. Use the official instructions and channels to check whether a service attracts any prescribed charge.

### What if my bank has no complaint number?

Ask again using a verifiable channel, keep proof of your first complaint and explain the missing reference when you seek help through official consumer-protection channels.

### Can I file a complaint against a wallet or fintech?

It depends on the licence and regulator of the particular provider. Identify the legal entity behind the product first and use the regulator's appropriate complaint route.

### Does the CBN handle failed transfers?

Complaints about regulated payment services may fall within the relevant consumer-protection process, but your provider should investigate the transfer first. Follow our [failed bank transfer action plan](/bank-transfer-failed-but-debited-in-nigeria/).

## Final checklist

Before escalating, make sure you can answer: *What happened? When? How much? Which institution? What reference? When did I complain? What did the bank say? What remedy am I requesting?*

That clarity—not the length of your message—usually makes a financial complaint easier to investigate.
