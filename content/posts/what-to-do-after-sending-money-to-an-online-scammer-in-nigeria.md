You send ₦85,000 for a product on social media. The supposed seller blocks you the moment you share the receipt. The transfer succeeded, but nothing has been delivered.

**Act quickly, and avoid anyone selling a guaranteed reversal.** A transfer you approved because someone lied to you is not the same as a transfer that failed due to a banking error. The distinction determines how you should report the case.

If money moved without your consent, see [unauthorised debits](/what-to-do-after-an-unauthorized-debit-in-nigeria/). If you mistyped an account while paying a legitimate person, use [our wrong-account recall guide](/how-to-reverse-money-sent-to-wrong-account-in-nigeria/).

## What to do in the first hour

### Save the original evidence

Keep the seller's profile URL, messages, promised goods or services, bank account details, transfer receipt, date and amount. Export the chat if possible. Make screenshots that show the sender and timestamp.

Write a short timeline: when you discovered the offer, when payment was requested, when you paid and what happened afterwards. Do not create false messages or change the originals.

### Contact the institution that sent the money

Open its verified app or call its official support line. Say:

> I believe I was deceived into authorising a transfer of ₦[amount] to [receiving bank/account] on [date]. The reference is [reference]. Please open an urgent suspected-fraud complaint, liaise with the receiving institution where possible and tell me what recall or investigation steps are available. Please provide a tracking number.

Ask for a written record. A successful transfer cannot always be taken back merely because the sender alleges fraud. Available balance, lawful powers and other factors may matter.

### Protect other accounts

If you typed a bank password into a strange website, shared a one-time code or installed unfamiliar software, contact your bank immediately about appropriate security controls. Change exposed passwords from a trusted device.

If you simply paid an unfamiliar merchant by bank transfer, you should monitor your account but not automatically assume your credentials were stolen.

## Why the type of dispute matters

| What happened | The right report |
| --- | --- |
| Bank debited you but no beneficiary received the transfer | [Failed transfer](/bank-transfer-failed-but-debited-in-nigeria/) |
| You entered a valid but wrong account number | [Wrong-account transfer](/how-to-reverse-money-sent-to-wrong-account-in-nigeria/) |
| You selected the account and paid after a deceptive promise | **Suspected scam or fraud** |
| You did not initiate the debit | [Unauthorised transaction](/what-to-do-after-an-unauthorized-debit-in-nigeria/) |

Do not label an authorised but deceptive payment as a network failure. Accurate facts make the investigation easier.

## Who should receive a fraud report?

The [CBN's fraud and scam awareness advice](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/supervision/cpdfraudandscam.html) encourages customers to notify banks promptly and contact appropriate authorities.

You can check the official [Economic and Financial Crimes Commission website](https://www.efcc.gov.ng) or relevant Nigerian Police channels for current reporting arrangements. Do not pay private agents who promise to file a government report or freeze an account for you.

If your complaint concerns how a regulated bank handled your case, [the CBN consumer complaints process](/how-to-report-a-bank-to-cbn-in-nigeria/) may also be appropriate. It is not a substitute for reporting suspected criminal deception.

## What documents should you attach?

A concise bundle can include the payment reference, receipt, sender and recipient institutions, original offer, conversation, the date you realised the problem and the bank's complaint number.

Send personal account details securely to the institution investigating the case. Do **not** publish the recipient's bank details, photographs or alleged identity on social media. A factual report to the authorities is different from public accusation.

If other people claim to be victims, keep their information private unless they choose to make their own report.

## How long will recovery take?

There is no universal guaranteed refund period for a voluntary transfer induced by deception. A failed banking transaction can have technical reversal deadlines; a fraudulent payment that successfully reached another account is different.

Ask the bank what action was taken, whether the receiving institution responded and when you should expect the next written update. Preserve the complaint reference.

When follow-up produces no answer, escalate the *handling of the complaint* through official regulatory routes rather than accepting vague promises indefinitely.

## Avoid the recovery scam

After the first scam, fraudsters may claim they can unlock your money if you pay an extra fee, share an OTP or install a remote-access app. Do not send more money to recover the first payment.

Be cautious of people pretending to be bank or government investigators on WhatsApp. See [fake bank customer-service warning signs](/how-to-identify-fake-bank-customer-service-numbers-in-nigeria/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a bank reverse a successful scam transfer?

It may be able to investigate or seek a return, but the outcome is fact-dependent. No legitimate adviser can promise every successful credit will be reversed.

### Should I report immediately or wait until tomorrow?

Contact your sending institution immediately. Delay can make investigation and recovery more difficult.

### Is a screenshot enough?

It is useful, but add the original transaction reference, full timeline, relevant messages and formal bank complaint number.

### Do I have to reveal my OTP for a refund?

No. A request for your OTP, PIN or banking password is a serious warning sign.

## Final takeaway

**Save evidence, report promptly, and use official bank and law-enforcement channels.** Recovery may or may not be possible, but you can avoid making the situation worse by refusing second-payment demands and keeping your information secure.
