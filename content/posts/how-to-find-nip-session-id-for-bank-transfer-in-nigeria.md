A seller says the bank transfer never arrived. Your app shows *successful*. Customer support asks for the NIP session ID, but all you see on the receipt is a short reference number. Where is this ID, and do you need it before the bank can investigate?

**NIP** means **NIBSS Instant Payment**, an account-based real-time transfer service operated by the Nigeria Inter-Bank Settlement System. A transaction passing through this system can have technical identifiers used to reconcile sending and receiving institutions. The [NIBSS description of NIP](https://nibss-plc.com.ng/nibss-instant-payment/) explains the service, but your own bank determines what identifiers its app or receipt actually displays.

This guide is about **finding the trace identifier**, not guessing whether the transfer failed. For the dispute procedure, read [transfer failed but account debited](/bank-transfer-failed-but-debited-in-nigeria/).

## First, don't confuse these numbers

| Label you might see | What it usually means |
| --- | --- |
| Bank transaction reference | Your bank or wallet's reference for its own records |
| Receipt number | An identifier associated with a generated receipt |
| NIP session ID | An identifier used on the interbank NIP transaction path |
| Beneficiary account number | Where you instructed the money to go; **not** the session ID |
| Complaint reference | The support ticket created after you report the problem |

A receipt may show more than one of these. They are **not always interchangeable**. Different banks label them differently, and a payment not routed through NIP may not have a NIP session ID at all.

## Where to look for the NIP session ID

### 1. Open the original transaction in your banking app

Go to transaction history, tap the specific transfer and look for **Details**, **Receipt**, **Reference**, **Session ID** or **Download**. Some apps include a long reference in the downloaded receipt that does not appear on the first transaction screen.

Do not trust a screenshot forwarded by another person as your only evidence. Open your own bank's record.

### 2. Check the email receipt or official statement

If your bank sends an email with transfer details, inspect it for a session ID or transaction reference. Account statements sometimes show a different identifier from the push notification.

Avoid searching through the email of an unrelated person or sharing a full statement just to obtain one reference.

### 3. Ask your bank directly

If the identifier isn't shown, contact your sending bank through its verified channels. A clear request is:

> I need the NIP session ID or interbank trace reference for a transfer made on [date] at [time] from my account to [receiving bank], amount ₦[amount]. My app's receipt reference is [reference]. Please give me the exact session ID or confirm whether this payment was sent through NIP.

You do **not** need to invent a 30-digit number from the transaction time. If your bank says the ID is unavailable, ask for a written trace status and a complaint number.

## When the receipt says successful but the receiver has no credit

The receiving bank may need an interbank identifier to trace the payment. The **sender's bank** is usually the best starting point because it has the original debit instruction and the corresponding outgoing transaction.

Ask it to confirm whether the receiving institution accepted the payment, whether a reversal was initiated and what case number you should use for follow-ups.

Our [failed-transfer guide](/bank-transfer-failed-but-debited-in-nigeria/) provides a decision tree for these outcomes. A *successful* screen alone is not definitive evidence that the intended recipient saw a credit.

## Can I check a NIP session ID myself online?

Be careful with websites or social-media accounts claiming to “recover” or “validate” NIP transactions. NIBSS publishes official information, but not every site that includes its name or logo is authorised to access your banking records.

Before using a service, confirm the URL and ask your bank whether it endorses that lookup. A service that demands a banking **password, card PIN or OTP** to show a transfer status is a strong warning sign.

Some banks provide transaction-status checks in their own apps. Use those first.

## What if I sent money to the wrong account?

The session ID may help locate the payment, but the **recall process is different**. If a valid account received money you authorised by mistake, a refund may require recipient cooperation or other formal action.

Read [our wrong-account transfer recall guide](/how-to-reverse-money-sent-to-wrong-account-in-nigeria/) instead of submitting a false failed-transfer complaint.

## Keep a privacy-safe record

Record the following in a note stored securely on your device:

1. Date and approximate local time.
2. Amount and sending institution.
3. Receiving bank and beneficiary name shown on your app.
4. Transaction or receipt reference.
5. NIP session ID, **if confirmed**.
6. Original screenshot or downloadable receipt.
7. Complaint number and update dates.

You can use our [free dispute evidence checklist](/resources/bank-dispute-checklist/) without entering any account credentials into a website.

If support requests proof of account ownership, use an official secure process. Do not upload full documents to public forums.

## FAQs

### Is the transaction reference the NIP session ID?

Not necessarily. Some apps show the technical ID directly; others show an internal bank reference. Ask the bank to confirm what the field represents.

### Can I use an SMS debit alert?

It can show the time and amount, but a complete bank transaction detail or receipt is better for tracing. Do not depend on the alert alone.

### What if my bank refuses to give the session ID?

Ask for a formal transaction trace and complaint tracking number. If your complaint remains unresolved, review the [CBN complaints process](/how-to-report-a-bank-to-cbn-in-nigeria/).

### How long is a NIP session ID?

Formats and visible representations can vary by provider or channel. Rather than guessing a length, ask the institution for the exact session ID tied to your transaction.

### Can the recipient find it?

The receiving institution may have its own reference for the incoming transaction. The sending bank is generally best placed to identify the outgoing interbank record.

## The bottom line

Your objective is not to memorise the format of a session ID. It is to **give the bank a traceable original payment**. Start with your receipt, ask for the genuine interbank ID if needed, and keep an official complaint reference until the dispute is resolved.
