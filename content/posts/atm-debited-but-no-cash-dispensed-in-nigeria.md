You ask an ATM for ₦40,000. The screen says *unable to dispense cash*. Your account balance falls by ₦40,000 anyway. A bystander says you should try again, but you are worried that you will lose another ₦40,000.

This is an **ATM non-dispense or partial-dispense dispute**. It is not the same as a [bank-to-bank transfer that failed](/bank-transfer-failed-but-debited-in-nigeria/). Different transaction rails, logs and CBN requirements may apply.

The most important recent source is the [CBN's March 2026 Guidelines on the Operations of Automated Teller Machines in Nigeria](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2026/CCD/ATM%20Guidelines.pdf). It supersedes the ATM-operation provisions of earlier payment-channel rules. Older online articles may still quote a single 72-hour timeframe from historic FAQs, so check which guidance they mean.

## First, confirm what the machine actually did

There are three situations:

| What happened | What you should report |
| --- | --- |
| You were debited ₦40,000 and received nothing | Non-dispense error for the full ₦40,000 |
| You were debited ₦40,000 but received ₦20,000 | Partial dispense; claim the missing ₦20,000 |
| The machine displayed an error but your account was not debited | Monitor the statement; there may be no funds to reverse |

Never exaggerate the cash shortfall. The investigation may use the ATM’s transaction log, cash balancing and available surveillance evidence.

If the machine retains your card or appears tampered with, contact your bank promptly using a verified number or app. Do not accept “help” from a stranger who asks for your PIN.

## What the CBN's 2026 ATM reversal rules say

The [March 2026 CBN guidelines](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2026/CCD/ATM%20Guidelines.pdf), section 11, distinguish two cases:

- **On-us:** using a card at an ATM operated by the bank that holds the underlying account. Failed transactions should reverse instantly. Where an instant reversal fails because of technical problems, the manual reversal timeline must not exceed **24 hours**.
- **Not-on-us:** using your card at another bank's ATM. The guidelines state that refunds on failed transactions must not exceed **48 hours**. The section also mentions shorter operational benchmarks for certain reversal processing steps, but these should not be confused with a guaranteed customer refund clock for every disputed case.

These rules concern **verified failed transactions**, not a guarantee that every disputed debit will be resolved in seconds. Disputed facts can require investigation; ask the bank why any deadline has not been met and obtain a written case number.

The CBN also directs ATM deployers to reconcile partial and non-dispense errors and put mechanisms in place to initiate refunds without waiting for the cardholder to complain.

## Four things to do at the ATM

### 1. Do not immediately repeat a large withdrawal

Check whether the first debit has reversed before deciding to retry. If you do try again because cash is urgently needed, treat the second attempt as a distinct transaction and preserve both references.

### 2. Write down the terminal details

Photograph the *terminal ID or location marker* if it is safe and permitted, not other customers. Record the exact time, address, institution operating the ATM, amount requested, actual cash received, card-issuing bank and transaction reference.

If a receipt prints, keep it. A bank statement or app transaction detail is usually stronger evidence than an SMS alert alone.

### 3. Check your account history

Look for the original debit and any separate reversal entry. Occasionally, reversal alerts arrive late. A pending reversal is not a completed credit until it shows in the transaction history.

### 4. Raise a formal dispute with your bank

Contact your **card issuer** through its official customer support or branch. The ATM deployer may also have a helpdesk displayed at the terminal, but your card issuer should record your claim and coordinate with the other institution as appropriate.

Ask for the complaint/reference number. Use the relevant dates to monitor the reversal deadline.

## A copyable ATM reversal complaint

> Subject: ATM non-dispense / partial-dispense dispute
>
> On [date] at [time], I attempted a cash withdrawal of ₦[amount requested] at an ATM operated by [bank/operator] at [location]. My account with [issuing bank] was debited ₦[amount debited], but I received ₦[actual cash received]. The unreceived amount is ₦[difference]. Transaction reference: [reference].
>
> Please investigate the terminal and transaction logs, reverse the unreceived amount under applicable CBN ATM guidelines and provide a complaint tracking number and expected update date.

Never include your PIN, password or OTP in this message. For a printable list of useful evidence, use our [bank-transfer complaint checklist](/resources/bank-dispute-checklist/).

## What if the ATM belongs to a different bank?

That is a **not-on-us** case. You can give the ATM owner's support desk the location and transaction details, but report to your **own bank** as card issuer too. Ask it to open a dispute with the ATM acquirer instead of sending you in circles.

The March 2026 CBN document assigns obligations to acquirers and deployers; it does not mean you must personally identify all parties in the payment-processing chain.

If your own bank says no record exists, request the precise transaction ID and statement entry. If the other institution says it dispensed cash, ask for the dispute escalation route rather than assuming the claim is over.

## When should you escalate?

If the bank has not reversed a confirmed failed transaction within the applicable timeframe, follow up immediately with the original complaint number. If the dispute remains unresolved after the institution's complaints process, use [our CBN escalation guide](/how-to-report-a-bank-to-cbn-in-nigeria/) and the official [CBN complaints portal](https://complaintsportal.cbn.gov.ng/).

The **technical reversal deadline** and the **general regulatory complaint-escalation timeline** are different things. Do not mistake a guideline requiring a fast reversal for an instruction to wait weeks before reporting the error to your bank.

## FAQs

### What if I received some cash, but not the full amount?

Report a **partial-dispense error** and state the exact missing amount. The bank should reconcile the ATM's records.

### Is the old 72-hour rule still applicable?

Many old guides cite a 72-hour benchmark. The CBN issued dedicated ATM guidelines in **March 2026**, with explicit on-us and not-on-us reversal rules. Use the latest official instrument for your dispute.

### Can I claim extra cash for the inconvenience?

Do not assume automatic compensation. The refund of the unreceived cash and a claim for other losses are separate questions. Ask the institution for its formal policy and seek appropriate advice where needed.

### Should I try the same ATM again?

Only if you understand the risk of a second debit and the first transaction's status. A different functioning ATM may be safer if you urgently need cash.

## Bottom line

Save the evidence, distinguish **same-bank from other-bank ATM**, open a traceable dispute and follow the March 2026 CBN reversal requirements. The card issuer and ATM deployer should investigate; you should not need to surrender your credentials or pay a third party to recover a genuine non-dispense debit.
