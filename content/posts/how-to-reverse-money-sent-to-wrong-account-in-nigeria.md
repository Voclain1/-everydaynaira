One wrong digit can send your money to somebody else. It might be a rent payment, a school fee or money meant for a supplier. The amount leaves your account, the receiving bank accepts it, and the name on the receipt is not the person you meant to pay.

A wrong-account transfer needs a different response from a [failed transfer with a debit](/bank-transfer-failed-but-debited-in-nigeria/). **If you authorised a transfer to the account that was displayed, the bank cannot always reverse a completed credit on demand.**

That does not mean recovery is hopeless. It means speed, evidence and the formal bank-to-bank recall process matter.

## What to do immediately after sending to the wrong account

### 1. Report the mistake to your sending institution

Open your bank's verified support channel or visit the branch. Tell them:

- The exact time, amount and transfer reference.
- The bank and account number you selected.
- The beneficiary you actually intended to pay.
- Whether you know the person who received the money.
- Whether the app displayed a beneficiary name before you confirmed.

Ask the bank to **log an erroneous-transfer recall request**, not merely note a general complaint. Request the case number and written confirmation that the recall has been initiated.

### 2. Preserve your original instruction

Download the transfer receipt and save any screenshots of the entry screen, beneficiary name and transaction details you have. Be accurate about what happened; do not describe a customer-entry mistake as a system error if you selected the wrong account yourself.

This distinction can affect the bank's obligations and the options available for recovering the money.

### 3. If you know the recipient, request a safe refund

If the transfer was to a friend, colleague or familiar vendor, politely ask them to contact their bank and arrange a return through the bank's verified process.

Do not publish their account details online, threaten them or engage strangers who promise to “freeze” the money for a fee. If you receive a call claiming to be from the bank, end it and call the bank's official number yourself.

## Can a bank reverse a wrong transfer without the recipient's permission?

**Not automatically in every case.** A completed payment to a genuine account is different from a failed payment or a bank-generated duplicate. A refund may require the recipient's cooperation, an investigation, or another lawful process depending on the facts.

The CBN has publicly hosted a 2018 document describing separate procedures for **bank error** and **customer error**; the available copy is watermarked *draft*. We therefore do not treat that copy alone as proof of a currently operative, unconditional reversal rule. Ask your bank what final regulation and dispute process it is applying to your case.

A practical way to frame the request is: **“Please notify the receiving institution, seek voluntary return of the funds and tell me what formal dispute or recovery process applies if the beneficiary refuses.”**

The receiving bank may not disclose the other person's confidential account information directly to you.

## What if the recipient refuses to return the money?

Ask the sending bank to confirm in writing whether it contacted the receiving bank, what response it received and what escalation is available.

Depending on the circumstances, the institution may investigate, involve its fraud or legal team, or advise you to obtain independent legal help. A court order might be relevant in contested cases, but it is not an automatic first step and should not be presented as a guaranteed route to a refund.

If the transaction was genuinely fraudulent—for example, someone tricked you into sending funds to an impersonator—make that clear immediately and consider reporting it to the police or the appropriate fraud authority. See [our guide to online scams](/online-scams-in-nigeria/) and [unauthorised account debits](/what-to-do-after-an-unauthorized-debit-in-nigeria/).

## Three situations that look similar but are not

| Situation | What you should report | What you should expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You typed an incorrect recipient account and confirmed it | Customer-initiated wrong-account transfer | A recall request and possible recipient cooperation |
| The bank transferred twice or to details different from your instruction | Possible bank or processing error | An investigation of the bank's handling and a different correction procedure |
| You never approved the transfer at all | Potentially unauthorised transaction or fraud | Immediate security action, fraud report and formal dispute |

The wrong label can waste valuable time. Your bank needs to know which of these happened.

## Sample recall message

> Subject: Urgent request to recall erroneous transfer
>
> On [date] at [time], I transferred ₦[amount] from [sending institution] to account [recipient account number] at [receiving bank]. I intended to pay [intended beneficiary], but I made an error in the destination details. Reference: [session ID/reference]. Please open a formal transfer-recall case with the receiving institution, confirm the tracking number and advise me of the relevant consent and dispute process. I have attached the transaction receipt and can provide further evidence securely.

Avoid sending a BVN, OTP, password or account login details by email.

## How long do wrong-transfer recalls take?

There is **no universal guaranteed recovery time**. A quick voluntary refund may resolve a mistake rapidly; a contested case, unavailable balance or unresolved receiving-bank investigation may take significantly longer.

Request a written update and the complaint reference. If your institution fails to address the complaint, you can follow the [CBN's published escalation process](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/supervision/cpdcomgt.html). It ordinarily asks that you report to the bank first and allow about two weeks for complaint resolution before escalating.

For the required evidence and [CBN complaint procedure](/how-to-report-a-bank-to-cbn-in-nigeria/), see our companion guide.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can the receiving bank simply take the money back?

Do not assume so. The appropriate mechanism depends on whether the transaction was initiated by the customer, caused by bank error or connected to fraud, and on the lawful basis for any reversal.

### Is a screenshot enough to recover my money?

It helps but it is not enough by itself. Keep the bank reference, beneficiary details and the sending bank's formal complaint number. A clear transaction trail improves the investigation.

### What if the bank asks me to call the recipient?

If you know the recipient, a courteous approach may help. If you do not know them, insist on a bank-mediated process and avoid public attempts to identify or shame the account holder.

### What if I was debited but no one received the funds?

That is a [failed or delayed transfer](/bank-transfer-failed-but-debited-in-nigeria/), not a mistaken successful payment. Ask the sending institution to trace it and reverse a confirmed failure.

## Prevent the next error

Before confirming a transfer, read the **recipient name and bank** on the confirmation screen, not only the digits. Save verified beneficiaries instead of retyping account details. For unusual or large payments, consider making a small test transfer **only if the added charges and inconvenience make sense**.

The best chance of recovery comes from acting promptly and using the banks' official channels. None of the steps above guarantees a refund, but they make sure your case is recorded and handled as the right kind of problem.

## Was it actually a scam rather than a typing error?

If you deliberately paid the account because someone misrepresented a product or identity, read [what to do after sending money to an online scammer](/what-to-do-after-sending-money-to-an-online-scammer-in-nigeria/) instead. A suspected fraud report differs from a straightforward wrong-account recall.
