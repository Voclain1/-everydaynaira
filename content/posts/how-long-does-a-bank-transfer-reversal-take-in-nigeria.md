You sent money. The debit alert came in. But the person did not receive it. Or you went to the ATM, the machine took your money, and no cash came out.

Now you are staring at your balance and asking the same question everyone asks: how long will it take for my money to come back?

The honest answer is: it depends on what kind of transfer it was. Some reversals happen in minutes. Some take two days. And one kind — money sent to the wrong account — is not a reversal at all, and it takes much longer.

## First, know which problem you have

Not every "my money is gone" is the same problem. The fix and the timeline depend on which one it is.

**A failed transfer.** You sent money, your account was debited, but the money never reached the other person. The transfer failed somewhere in the middle. This is the most common case, and it is the one this guide is mainly about.

**A debit without cash at the ATM.** You tried to withdraw, the machine debited your account, but no cash came out. This is also a failed transaction, and the CBN has clear timelines for it.

**A transfer to the wrong account.** You typed the wrong account number, or picked the wrong name, and the money went to a stranger. This is different. The money did not fail — it arrived, just at the wrong place. Getting it back is a longer process, and it needs the other person's bank to help. Our guide to [how to reverse money sent to the wrong account in Nigeria](/how-to-reverse-money-sent-to-wrong-account-in-nigeria/) covers that one step by step.

If your case is the first or second one, keep reading.

## The short answer: the timeline table

| What happened | How long should it take? |
| --- | --- |
| ATM, your own bank's machine | Instant. If the instant reversal fails, by hand within 24 hours. |
| ATM, another bank's machine | Within 48 hours. |
| Transfer on your phone or app (NIP) that failed | Often minutes to a few hours. There is no single CBN timeline for this, so report it the same day. |
| Transfer to the wrong account | Days to weeks. It is a recall, not a reversal. |

Now the detail behind each row.

## ATM withdrawals: the CBN set the clock

This is the one case where the timeline is written down in black and white.

In March 2026, the CBN issued its Guidelines on the Operations of Automated Teller Machines in Nigeria. Section 11 of those guidelines deals with failed transactions, and it is very direct.

**On-us.** That means you used your card at an ATM run by your own bank — say, a GTBank card at a GTBank machine. A failed on-us transaction must reverse instantly. If the instant reversal fails because of a technical problem, the bank must complete the manual reversal within **24 hours**.

**Not-on-us.** That means you used your card at another bank's ATM. The guidelines say the refund must not take more than **48 hours**. When the machines work properly, the automatic reversal should take less than 5 minutes for on-us transactions, and less than 15 minutes for not-on-us ones.

So if you used your own bank's ATM on Monday morning and the money is still missing on Wednesday, your bank has already passed the limit the CBN set. When you complain, you are not begging for a favour. You are asking the bank to follow its own regulator's rule. For what to do at the machine itself, see [ATM debited but no cash dispensed in Nigeria](/atm-debited-but-no-cash-dispensed-in-nigeria/).

## Phone and app transfers: fast, but no single rule

Most transfers in Nigeria today do not go through an ATM. They go through your bank app, USSD, or a fintech app, over NIP — the instant payment system.

Here is the part people do not expect: the March 2026 CBN guidelines quoted above are about ATMs. They do not set a single reversal timeline for a failed NIP transfer.

What happens in practice is this. When a NIP transfer fails, the money often comes back on its own within minutes, or within a few hours at most. But "often" is not "always". Sometimes the transfer sits in a strange middle state — debited from your side, not credited on the other side — and nobody's system fixes it on its own. That is why you should not just wait and hope. Report it the same day.

One detail that helps your bank find the transfer fast: the NIP session ID. Every NIP transfer has one, and it is the number your bank uses to trace the money. Our guide explains [how to find your NIP session ID for a bank transfer in Nigeria](/how-to-find-nip-session-id-for-bank-transfer-in-nigeria/). Keep it with your complaint.

## What to do, stage by stage

### Day 1: report it, and get a reference number

Do not wait to see if the money "will come back on its own". Report it the same day, through your bank's official channel — the app, the customer care line, or the branch.

Tell them the date and time, the amount, your account number and the account you were sending to, the NIP session ID if you can find it, and whether it was an ATM withdrawal or a transfer.

Then ask for one thing: **a reference number for your complaint**. This is the most important part of day one. Without a reference number, your complaint is just a phone call. With it, you have proof that you reported it, and a number to quote every time you follow up.

Write the reference number down. Take a screenshot of the alert and the failed transaction. Keep the receipt, if the ATM gave you one.

If the money comes back within hours, fine — the matter is closed. If it does not, you are ready for the next stage.

### Day 2 to 3: follow up in writing

If the money has not returned by the second day, follow up — and this time, do it in writing. Send an email, or use the bank's complaint form inside the app. State the facts again, quote your reference number, and quote the timeline that applies to you:

- "This was an on-us ATM transaction. The CBN guidelines say the reversal should be instant, or within 24 hours if done by hand."
- "This was a not-on-us ATM transaction. The CBN guidelines say the refund must not exceed 48 hours."

Keep the tone calm and short. You are not arguing. You are putting the facts on record. Ask for a date by which the money will be back in your account.

### After 48 hours: escalate inside the bank

For an ATM case, 48 hours is the outer limit for a not-on-us refund, and 24 hours for on-us. If that time has passed and your money is still missing, ask to escalate. Ask for a supervisor. Ask for the complaint to be moved to the bank's resolution team.

At this point, also check the basics again. Is the money truly missing, or did it return quietly without an alert? Check your full statement, not just the alerts. Sometimes a reversal lands without a new message, and people keep chasing money that is already back.

### If the bank will not act: complain to the CBN

If your bank has had a fair chance and the money is still not back, you can take the matter to the CBN. This is not a shortcut — do it after you have reported to your bank and given them time to act. The CBN expects you to have tried your bank first.

Our guide to [how to report a bank to the CBN in Nigeria](/how-to-report-a-bank-to-cbn-in-nigeria/) walks through the process. The short version: you will need your bank's reference number, the dates, the amount, and your evidence.

## Why do reversals take time at all?

A transfer touches several hands: your bank, the other person's bank, and the switch in the middle. When everything works, the money moves in seconds and everyone agrees. When one hand fails, the two sides disagree — your bank thinks the money left, the other bank never saw it arrive — and the systems have to agree again on who holds the money. That is why some reversals are instant and others drag. None of this is your fault, and none of it is a reason for a bank to keep your money without explaining what is happening.

## Three mistakes that make it worse

**Waiting a week before reporting.** The sooner you report, the easier the money is to trace. A complaint made on the same day is a live transaction. A complaint made a week later is an investigation.

**Sharing your details with "helpers".** After a failed transfer, you may get calls or messages from people who claim they can "fast-track" your refund. They cannot. No real bank officer will ask for your PIN, OTP or password to reverse a transfer. Anyone who asks is running a scam — see [how to identify fake bank customer service numbers in Nigeria](/how-to-identify-fake-bank-customer-service-numbers-in-nigeria/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a bank transfer reversal take in Nigeria?

For a failed ATM withdrawal, the CBN guidelines say: instant for your own bank's machine (or within 24 hours by hand if the instant reversal fails), and within 48 hours for another bank's machine. For a failed transfer on your phone or app, there is no single CBN timeline — most reverse within minutes or hours, but report it the same day and get a reference number.

### My transfer failed two days ago and the money is still gone. What do I do?

Follow up in writing with your bank, quote your reference number, and quote the timeline that applies. For an ATM case, the bank is already past the CBN limit. Ask for a date when the money will return, and ask to escalate if you do not get one.

### Is a transfer to the wrong account the same as a failed transfer?

No. A failed transfer never arrived. A transfer to the wrong account did arrive — at the wrong place. Getting it back needs the receiving bank's help, so it takes days to weeks, not hours. It is covered in [how to reverse money sent to the wrong account in Nigeria](/how-to-reverse-money-sent-to-wrong-account-in-nigeria/).

### Can the bank refuse to reverse my money?

A bank cannot keep money from a transfer that genuinely failed. If the transfer did not complete, the money is yours and it must come back. What a bank can do is take time to confirm the failure — which is why the CBN set the 24-hour and 48-hour limits for ATM cases.

### What details should I keep for my complaint?

The date and time, the amount, both account numbers, the NIP session ID if it was a transfer, the ATM location if it was a withdrawal, screenshots of the debit alert, and your bank's complaint reference number. Keep them all in one place until the money is back.

### Will I get the money back, or just a promise?

In most failed-transfer cases, the money does come back — the systems are built to reverse a transfer that did not complete. The danger is not that the money is lost for good. The danger is that nobody chases it. That is why the reference number and the written follow-up matter: they turn a quiet failure into a complaint that has to be answered.

## The bottom line

A failed transfer is stressful, but it is a common problem with a known fix. For an ATM withdrawal, the CBN has set the clock: instant, or 24 hours, for your own bank's machine; 48 hours for another bank's. For a transfer on your phone, report it on day one, get a reference number, follow up in writing, and escalate to the CBN if your bank will not act.
