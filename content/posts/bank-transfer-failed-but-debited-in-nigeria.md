You send ₦48,000 for an urgent purchase. Your bank alerts you that the money has left your account, but the seller says nothing arrived. The app alternates between *pending* and *successful*. You are worried that if you send it again, you could lose another ₦48,000.

The first thing to understand is that **a debit, a completed transfer and a recipient credit are three different pieces of information**. You need to know which part actually happened before deciding what to do.

This guide covers the common case of an attempted Nigerian bank or wallet transfer where the sender is debited but the recipient has not received value. If you entered the **wrong but valid account number**, use our separate [wrong-account transfer recovery guide](/how-to-reverse-money-sent-to-wrong-account-in-nigeria/). If you never initiated the debit, start with [unauthorised transactions](/what-to-do-after-an-unauthorized-debit-in-nigeria/) instead.

## First: work out which transfer problem you have

| What you can confirm | What it might mean | Your next move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sender debited; app says failed; receiver has no credit | Failed payment awaiting reversal | Check for an automatic refund; report it to your sending bank |
| Sender debited; app says pending | Status not yet final | Ask your sending bank for a status trace or NIP session ID |
| Sender says successful; receiver finds no credit | Banks disagree about settlement or posting | Sender asks for transaction trace; receiver checks statement with receiving bank |
| Sender debited twice for one intended payment | Duplicate debit or duplicate transfer | Report both references, not just the amount |
| Money reached a different account that you selected by mistake | Customer input error, not a failed transfer | Request a recall immediately through your sending bank |

A screenshot of a debit alert is useful evidence. It is **not final proof that a beneficiary received the money**.

## What to do in the first 30 minutes

### 1. Check both full statements

Open the transaction details in your app, not just the SMS alert. Look for the transfer's status, date, timestamp, beneficiary name, bank and reference or session ID. Ask the recipient to check their **account transaction history**, including delayed credits, rather than relying solely on an alert.

If the app shows an unresolved or pending payment, do not automatically retry. A retry may create a second successful transfer if the first finishes later.

### 2. Save a small evidence file

Keep the receipt or reference, amount, sending and receiving institutions, the account number shown on the original instruction, the exact timestamp and screenshots of the current status. A NIP session ID can help the banks trace the transaction, although the format and availability differ between providers.

You can redact unrelated account information when sharing screenshots. Never send a PIN, password, CVV, OTP or full card details to someone offering to help.

### 3. Contact the institution that sent the money

Use the support option in your bank's own app, its verified website or an official branch. State clearly that **you were debited, the beneficiary has no confirmed credit, and you need a trace or reversal investigation**.

Ask these four questions:

- Has the transfer settled successfully or failed?
- Is a reversal already pending, and what is its reference?
- Can you provide the session ID or dispute tracking number?
- What is the next update date, and who owns the case?

Keep a record of every response. Do not rely on a vague promise that “network will reverse it”.

## How long does a failed transfer reversal take in Nigeria?

[NIBSS explains](https://nibss-plc.com.ng/how-cbn-revolutionized-payment-systems-in-nigeria/) that banks are generally expected to reverse genuinely failed electronic transfers **instantly or within 24 hours**, with a longer dispute-resolution process for exceptions. This is a useful benchmark, **not a guarantee that every individual case will be refunded in exactly one day**.

The important distinction is whether the money actually reached the beneficiary's bank. A confirmed successful transfer may require a reconciliation rather than a simple reversal. When a bank is investigating, ask for a case-specific timeframe and a traceable explanation of any delay.

If 24 hours have passed and neither a recipient credit nor a reversal is confirmed, **open or follow up a formal dispute** instead of waiting indefinitely. If you need urgent goods or services, explain the uncertainty to the person you were paying before you initiate any second payment.

## A bank complaint message you can copy

> Subject: Dispute — account debited but beneficiary not credited
>
> I initiated a transfer of ₦[amount] on [date] at [time] from [sending institution] to [receiving institution]. My account was debited, but the beneficiary has not confirmed receipt. The transaction reference/session ID is [reference]. Please confirm the final transaction status, investigate with the receiving institution and reverse the debit if the transfer failed. Kindly issue a complaint tracking number and tell me when to expect a written update.

Do not include your password or transaction PIN. If you need help separating normal bank fees from an unusual debit, see [our bank-charges explainer](/bank-charges-in-nigeria-explained/).

## What if the sending bank says “successful”?

Ask the bank to **trace the payment with the receiving institution**, including the original transaction reference. The recipient can also ask their bank to search their statement or incoming transfers using the reference and date.

It is possible for an alert to arrive late, for the receiving account to have a restriction, or for an intermediate transfer status to be misunderstood. Do not accuse either party of fraud without evidence. Ask the banks to confirm exactly where the transfer ended.

If the receiving bank says it cannot find the payment, have that response recorded in the case. A successful app screen should not be the end of the investigation if the beneficiary genuinely received nothing.

## How to escalate to the CBN

The [CBN's complaints guidance](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/supervision/cpdcomgt.html) requires you to **complain to your institution first** and generally allows **about two weeks** for resolution of an ordinary complaint. Faster technical transaction benchmarks may apply to the underlying reversal; the two-week period concerns escalation of the complaint process.

If the bank does not resolve it within the relevant period, follow [our step-by-step CBN complaint guide](/how-to-report-a-bank-to-cbn-in-nigeria/). Include the bank's complaint reference, transaction evidence, previous correspondence and a clear explanation of the remedy you seek. The CBN also provides an [online complaints portal](https://complaintsportal.cbn.gov.ng/).

A payment dispute is easier to handle when you can show the entire trail: original instruction, debit, recipient's missing credit, your first complaint and the institution's response.

## Common questions

### Can I get the money back if I was debited but the transfer failed?

Normally, a genuinely failed transfer should be reversed. But the bank must first establish whether the receiving institution actually applied the funds. Ask for a traceable case reference.

### Should I send the money again?

Not until you have established whether the original payment succeeded or was reversed, unless you deliberately accept the risk of temporarily paying twice.

### Is the sender's bank or the recipient's bank responsible?

Start with the institution that debited you. It can work with the beneficiary's bank and the payment system to investigate. The recipient may need to involve their bank if a successful inbound transfer is missing.

### Can someone on WhatsApp speed up my reversal?

Treat anyone asking for an OTP, PIN, payment or remote access to “unlock” your refund as suspicious. Use verified institutional channels only.

## The bottom line

A failed bank transfer is stressful, but the safest response is methodical: verify the status, protect the evidence, avoid a duplicate payment, lodge a formal case and escalate when necessary. The number that matters most is not a promised refund hour; it is your **transaction and complaint reference**, which allows the problem to be followed up.

For situations involving a genuine error in the destination account, the process is different. Read [how to recall a wrong-account transfer](/how-to-reverse-money-sent-to-wrong-account-in-nigeria/).
