You made sales this week. Payments came in. Your account moved. But somehow, you feel no different from last week.

Maybe the money went into rent. Or stock. Or delivery. Or airtime, again. Or that emergency repair you did not plan for. You cannot say exactly, because nothing is written down and memory is not reliable enough for this.

This is one of the most common money problems Nigerian small business owners face. It is not that the business is not working. It is that the cash keeps moving and no one is watching where it goes. By month end, there is nothing to show for the sales, no clear picture of what was spent, and no way to tell if the business actually made a profit or just stayed busy.

Tracking your business expenses properly is what fixes that. It is not complicated accounting. It is simply the habit of writing down what your business is spending before the money disappears and the memory fades.

This guide explains what to track, why it matters, and how to do it in a way that actually fits your life as a small business owner in Nigeria.

## What counts as a business expense?

A business expense is any money your business spends to operate or keep running. If the payment would not exist without the business, it is likely a business expense.

That includes:

- stock, raw materials, or goods you buy to resell or use in your work
- packaging materials
- transport costs related to business activity
- delivery fees
- airtime and data used for business calls, customer contact, or marketing
- internet subscription if the business uses it
- generator fuel or electricity costs for your workspace
- rent for your shop, market stall, or workspace
- bank transfer charges on business payments
- repairs on equipment the business depends on
- wages or payments to anyone helping you
- subscriptions to online tools or platforms your business uses

Some of these costs feel small. Twenty naira here, five hundred there. That is exactly why many business owners ignore them. But the small costs add up quickly, and when you stop tracking them, they silently eat into your profit.

## Why tracking small business expenses matters

**You cannot know your real profit without it.**

This is the most important reason. Revenue is not profit. If you collected ₦200,000 this month but spent ₦160,000 running the business, your actual profit is ₦40,000. If you did not track expenses, you might spend as if you made ₦200,000, and wonder why the account is always low.

Many small business owners make this mistake. They see money coming in and feel confident. But without knowing what went out, that confidence is built on incomplete information.

**You cannot price properly without it.**

Your prices need to cover your costs and leave something behind. If you do not know what your costs are, your prices are guesses. You might be selling at a loss and not realising it because the cash flow looks okay on the surface.

If you are managing income from a side hustle alongside other earnings, understanding your real costs becomes even more important. Our guide on [managing side hustle money](/how-to-pay-yourself-from-a-side-hustle/) covers how to keep those income streams separate and clear.

**You cannot spot waste without it.**

Once you start recording expenses, patterns show up. You might notice that delivery costs are eating a large share of every sale. Or that subscriptions you forgot about are renewing every month. Or that fuel for business trips is higher than expected. None of this is obvious until it is written down.

**You cannot plan cash flow without it.**

If you know which expenses come up every week, every month, or at irregular intervals, you can plan for them instead of being caught short. Business owners who track expenses stop being surprised by costs they should have seen coming.

**Tax and record-keeping readiness.**

If your business ever needs to show financial records — to a bank, a potential partner, or for tax purposes — you will need some form of expense history. Businesses registered with the CAC or operating at a certain scale may eventually have formal reporting requirements. Tracking expenses now builds that foundation without pressure.

## Common business expenses to track in Nigeria

Not all businesses have the same costs. But most Nigerian small business owners will recognise most of these:

**Stock and materials** Anything you buy to resell or use in producing what you sell. This is often the largest cost and the easiest to forget to record properly, especially when you pay cash at markets or from suppliers.

**Transport** Getting to customers, suppliers, markets, or business locations. Tricycle fares, bus fares, or fuel if you drive. These add up fast, especially in cities like Lagos and Abuja where transport is not cheap.

**Delivery costs** Dispatch riders, logistics companies, and delivery apps. If you run an online business or regularly ship goods, this is a major line item.

**Airtime and data** The cost of calling customers, receiving orders, managing social media, and staying reachable. This is easy to forget because it comes out of a personal phone — which is exactly why it needs to be tracked separately.

**Internet subscription** If your business depends on being online, the data or broadband cost belongs in your expense records.

**Fuel and generator costs** Power supply is unpredictable across most of Nigeria. If your business runs on a generator, the fuel cost is a real business expense, not a personal one.

**Rent** Whether for a shop, market stall, storage space, or workspace. This is usually fixed and easy to plan for, but it still needs to be in the records.

**Packaging** Bags, boxes, labels, tape, nylon, stickers. These costs feel minor per unit but accumulate quickly, especially for businesses fulfilling many orders.

**Bank charges and transfer fees** Every USSD transfer, mobile banking charge, or ATM fee related to business payments is a cost. If you are making many transfers a week, this number can surprise you. If you want a clearer picture of how these charges work, our explainer on [bank transfer fees in Nigeria](/bank-charges-nigeria/) breaks them down.

**Support staff payments** If you have someone who helps run the business — a shop assistant, a delivery person, a cleaner — their pay is a business expense.

**Online tools and subscriptions** Design apps, social media scheduling tools, e-commerce platforms, accounting apps. Even small monthly fees should be recorded.

**Repairs and maintenance** A broken phone, a faulty sewing machine, a damaged cooling unit. These are irregular but real.

## How to track business expenses simply

You do not need accounting software to start. What you need is a reliable system, and the discipline to use it every day.

**Step 1: Record expenses immediately**

The moment you spend money on the business, write it down. Do not wait until evening. Do not rely on memory. Memory is comfortable with round numbers and forgets the small ones.

A small pocket notebook you carry with you works. A note on your phone works. What does not work is planning to remember.

**Step 2: Keep receipts where you can**

For purchases where a receipt or invoice is available, keep it. This gives you a record you can verify later. Not every expense in informal business settings will come with a receipt, but for supplier purchases, subscriptions, and formal services, ask for one.

**Step 3: Use a dedicated expense notebook or spreadsheet**

A physical notebook divided into categories is enough to start. You can also use a free spreadsheet — Google Sheets is accessible on any smartphone with a browser.

A simple layout:

| Date | Description | Category | Amount (₦) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 15 May | Market stock | Stock | 18,000 |
| 15 May | Dispatch rider | Delivery | 1,200 |
| 16 May | Airtime | Communication | 500 |

Keep categories consistent so you can compare week to week.

**Step 4: Separate business and personal spending**

This is where many small business owners go wrong. When business money and personal money mix in the same account or wallet, it becomes almost impossible to know what was actually spent on the business.

If you can, use a separate bank account for business income and payments. Even a separate mobile money wallet helps. The goal is a clear boundary. Our guide on [separating business and personal money](/how-to-separate-personal-and-business-money-in-nigeria/) explains how to set this up practically.

**Step 5: Record cash expenses too**

Bank transfers are easy to verify later. Cash payments are not. If you paid cash for stock at the market, fuel from a roadside seller, or daily supplies, that still needs to go into your expense records. Cash expenses are the most likely to disappear from your records if you are not deliberate about them.

**Step 6: Group expenses into categories**

Rather than a long random list, group costs by type: stock, transport, delivery, communication, utilities, packaging, and so on. This makes it easier to see which category is taking the most money and whether anything seems unusually high.

## Best tools for tracking small business expenses

**Notebook and pen** Still one of the most reliable tools for solo business owners, especially if you spend a lot of time in markets or places with poor internet. The discipline matters more than the tool.

**Google Sheets or Microsoft Excel** A basic spreadsheet with date, description, category, and amount columns is enough for most small businesses. You can review totals easily, add up categories, and spot trends. Google Sheets is free and works on any phone.

**Simple bookkeeping apps** There are a growing number of apps designed for African small businesses that allow you to record income and expenses on your phone without needing accounting knowledge. When evaluating any app, look for one that lets you record cash and transfer payments, works offline when needed, and does not require a subscription that costs more than the value it gives you. Ask other small business owners in your network what they use and whether it has been reliable.

**Avoid complex accounting software to start** Full accounting software with invoicing, payroll, and tax modules is more than most solo business owners need at the beginning. Start simple. You can upgrade the system as the business grows.

## How often you should review your expenses

**Every day:** Record expenses as they happen. This is not a review — it is simply the habit of writing things down before you forget.

**Every week:** Add up the week's expenses by category. Compare this week to last week. Look for anything unusually high or any costs you forgot to record until now.

**Every month:** Total the month's expenses. Calculate what the business actually made after costs. Identify the top expense categories. Ask whether any cost is higher than it should be, and whether prices need to be adjusted.

**Every quarter:** Look at three months together. Are any costs trending upward? Are there expenses that have grown without notice? Are there subscriptions or regular costs that no longer make sense for the business?

Monthly review is the minimum. Weekly review is better. For a growing business, both matter.

## Common mistakes to avoid

**Not recording small costs** Transport, airtime, packaging, and similar small costs feel too minor to bother with. But ₦500 a day in small forgotten costs is ₦15,000 a month. That is real money.

**Mixing personal and business money** This makes accurate tracking nearly impossible. Even if you cannot open a separate bank account immediately, a separate envelope for cash or a different mobile wallet makes a meaningful difference.

**Relying on bank transaction history alone** Bank records only show transfers and card payments. Cash payments will not appear there. If most of your spending is cash, your bank history tells you almost nothing about what the business actually spent.

**Recording inconsistently** Writing expenses down for three days and then stopping for a week means your records are incomplete. An incomplete record is almost as unhelpful as no record at all. The habit has to be daily.

**Forgetting irregular costs** Annual subscriptions, equipment repairs, and seasonal costs are easy to forget in monthly budgeting. When they arrive, they look like surprises. If you review your records regularly, you will start to notice patterns in when these costs appear.

**Not categorising expenses** A long list of transactions without categories is hard to interpret. Grouping expenses shows you where the money actually goes, which is the whole point of tracking.

## FAQ

**Do I need an accountant to track my business expenses?** Not to get started. Recording your daily expenses in a notebook or spreadsheet is something you can do yourself. An accountant becomes more useful when the business grows, when you need to file taxes, or when your records become complex enough to need professional organisation.

**What if I use cash for most of my business expenses?** Cash expenses need to be recorded immediately, because there is no bank record to fall back on. Keep a small notebook or use a note on your phone. Record the amount, what it was for, and the date, as soon as the money leaves your hand.

**How do I separate business expenses from personal ones?** The most reliable way is to have a separate bank account or mobile wallet for business transactions. If that is not immediately possible, keep a separate record for business spending and be strict about what you count. Mixed spending is one of the biggest sources of confusion for small business owners.

**Is there a free tool I can use to track expenses?** Google Sheets is free, accessible on any smartphone, and enough for most small businesses starting out. A simple table with date, description, category, and amount is all you need to begin.

**How do I know if my business is actually profitable?** Take your total revenue for the month and subtract all your business expenses for that same period. What remains is your gross profit. If it is a small number — or negative — your pricing, your costs, or both need attention.

**Should I track expenses even if my business is very small?** Yes. Especially if it is small. A small business with thin margins cannot afford to lose money to untracked costs. The smaller the business, the more each naira matters.

**What categories should I use for my expense records?** A useful starting set: Stock/Materials, Transport, Delivery, Communication (airtime and data), Utilities (fuel, electricity), Packaging, Rent, Bank Charges, Staff/Support, Subscriptions, and Repairs. Adjust based on what your business actually spends money on.

**Can expense tracking help me set better prices?** Yes. Once you know your real costs per unit or per service, you can work backwards to a price that covers those costs and leaves a profit margin. Many small businesses are underpriced simply because the owner has never added up all the costs properly.

## Conclusion

Running a business without tracking expenses is like trying to navigate somewhere new without checking where you are. Money is moving, sales are happening, but the full picture stays invisible.

You do not need to become an accountant. You need a reliable daily habit — record what the business spends, keep the records organised, and review them regularly. That is it. The tools can be as simple as a notebook. The categories can be simple. What matters is consistency.

Start with today. Write down what the business spent. Do it again tomorrow. Within a few weeks, you will have something no amount of guessing can give you: an honest picture of where the money is actually going — and a real basis for deciding what to do next.
