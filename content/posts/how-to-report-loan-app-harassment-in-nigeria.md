A loan-app collector threatens to send your photograph to everyone in your contact list. Some messages describe you as a criminal. You may owe a real loan, but you do not know what to do about the threats—or whether the lender can legally contact other people about your debt.

**Separate two issues immediately:** (1) whether you owe money under a valid agreement and (2) whether the lender or its agent is using abusive, deceptive or privacy-violating methods to collect it. One does not make the other acceptable.

Nigeria's [Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission](https://fccpc.gov.ng/about-us/faqs/) describes unfair digital debt collection and privacy abuse as consumer-protection concerns. Here is a practical approach focused on evidence and safety.

## What behaviour should you record?

Examples of conduct worth documenting include:

| Situation | Evidence to preserve |
| --- | --- |
| Threats to send a debt message to your family, employer or contacts | Original chat, screenshot with sender and time, call logs |
| Your photograph or personal information is shared without appropriate authority | Recipient's copy, group link or screenshot, context of publication |
| Collectors demand money to an unfamiliar personal account | Payment request, account name, official lender payment details |
| Calls repeatedly threaten violence or claim to be police without proof | Number, time, voicemail where lawfully retained, precise words |
| Collection amount changes without explanation | Original loan offer, schedule, fees and payment receipts |

Do not edit screenshots to make them more dramatic. Preserve the originals as a chronological record.

Do not publish the collector's phone number, the borrower's ID or other people's contact details on social media as retaliation.

## What the current FCCPC rules say

The FCCPC issued the [2025 DEON digital-lending regulations](https://fccpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/DIGITAL-CONSUMER-LENDING-2025-LATEST.pdf) to strengthen consumer disclosures, fair collection and privacy protections.

An April 2026 court order temporarily stopped implementation, but the [FCCPC announced on 20 July 2026 that the restraint was lifted](https://fccpc.gov.ng/fccpc-resumes-digital-lending-regulation/) and implementation had resumed. Older articles saying the entire framework remains suspended should be checked against that later official update.

You do not need to quote section numbers from memory to make a complaint. Describe the exact acts, dates, participants and harm. The regulator can decide which legal provisions apply.

## Step 1: protect yourself and others

If the messages suggest immediate danger, seek help from relevant law enforcement and people you trust. Avoid confronting an abusive agent in person.

Secure your phone, email and banking app. Review app permissions and remove access that is no longer necessary. Be aware that removing a permission after the fact **does not necessarily erase data already copied** by an app.

Do not share OTPs, PINs, passwords or remote-access codes with a collector—no matter what name they use.

## Step 2: identify the actual lender

Go back to the loan agreement and confirm the legal company name, support address, disbursement institution and loan reference. If the app or caller uses another name, record the discrepancy.

Check the legal company and product against the [FCCPC approved digital lenders register](https://fccpc.gov.ng/registration-of-digital-money-lenders/approvals-of-dmls/), using [our verification checklist](/how-to-check-if-a-loan-app-is-approved-in-nigeria/).

Do not assume every party with a similar trading name is the same company.

## Step 3: lodge a written complaint

Contact the lender through a verified support channel rather than responding to threatening numbers. Explain the conduct and ask the institution to stop improper contact and provide a written account of the loan.

> Subject: Complaint about abusive collection and disclosure of personal data
>
> I am a customer of [legal lender/app] with loan reference [reference]. On [date(s)], persons identifying themselves as your collection agents sent [describe the messages] and threatened to contact [persons, without exposing their private numbers].
>
> I request an investigation, cessation of abusive contact, written confirmation of the lawful balance and repayment channels, and clarification of any disclosure of my personal information. Please provide a complaint reference and written response.

A firm factual letter works better than an exchange of insults. Keep a copy of what you sent.

## Step 4: report to the FCCPC

The FCCPC provides an official [consumer complaint portal and guidance](https://fccpc.gov.ng/consumers/complaint-handling/). Its digital lending publications also identify **lenderstaskforce@fccpc.gov.ng** for reporting concerns about digital lenders.

Attach copies of the agreement and evidence of the collection behaviour. Explain whether the harm involved contact shaming, misleading charges, unlawful disclosure or threatening conduct.

Avoid sending your complete contact list to prove that your contacts were accessed. A smaller, relevant evidence package is usually sufficient.

## Step 5: consider a data-protection complaint

Where the central problem is misuse of your contacts, photographs or other personal information, the [Nigeria Data Protection Commission's breach/privacy portal](https://services.ndpc.gov.ng/breach/) provides an avenue to report alleged data-protection violations.

State what data was collected, how it was allegedly disclosed, when you learned of it and what evidence you have. A regulator's decision depends on facts and applicable law; a complaint is not an automatic damages award.

## What about the money you still owe?

Ask for the current breakdown of principal, agreed interest, authorised fees, previous payments and remaining balance. Do not let threats push you to transfer money to an unverified personal account.

If the debt is valid but you cannot pay immediately, communicate through official channels and ask whether a written repayment arrangement is available. Never promise a date you know you cannot meet.

The [loan interest guide](/how-loan-interest-works-in-nigeria/) and [dangerous loan warning signs](/signs-of-dangerous-loan-offers-every-nigerian-should-know/) can help you understand the written offer.

## FAQs

### Can a lender send messages to all my contacts?

A broad claim that every disclosure is lawful because an app requested contact permissions is unsafe. The actual consent, purpose and applicable rules matter; report suspected misuse with evidence.

### Will filing a complaint cancel my loan?

Not automatically. Harassment and the underlying debt should be addressed separately.

### What if collectors impersonate law enforcement?

Save the messages and consider reporting impersonation or threats to the appropriate authorities. Do not pay a stranger because a chat profile displays a uniform.

### Should I delete the app immediately?

Preserve contracts, repayment records, messages and privacy notices before removing access. If your device is compromised, prioritise safety and secure important accounts.

## The bottom line

Document the behaviour, identify the accountable lender, complain through verified channels and use the FCCPC/NDPC processes where appropriate. **Your dignity and privacy matter**, even when a repayment is disputed.
