Your house rent is due in March. It is November, and you have only a small amount saved. The landlord is unlikely to accept “I forgot to budget” as a payment plan.

House rent is one of those expenses that feels like an emergency because it is paid in a lump sum. But the date was predictable all along. The way to make it manageable is to build a **rent sinking fund**—money reserved for a specific future bill.

This guide uses Nigerian naira examples. They are calculations, not estimates of average rent in Lagos, Abuja or any other city.

## First calculate the entire rent target

Write down the expected renewal amount and any other unavoidable, properly disclosed costs you may need to pay. For a move, separately examine items such as transport, deposit arrangements, service charges or documented agency fees. The lawful and customary treatment of those fees depends on your location and agreement; do not assume one rule applies across Nigeria.

For an existing tenancy, avoid automatically padding a negotiated rent with speculative charges. A modest contingency may still help if the renewal amount is uncertain.

### Example: ₦720,000 annual rent plus ₦60,000 contingency

| Component | Target |
| --- | ---: |
| Expected annual rent | ₦720,000 |
| Optional contingency for a possible change or moving costs | ₦60,000 |
| **Total planning target** | **₦780,000** |

If the costs do not materialise, the ₦60,000 remains your money. It is not a recommendation to pay unverified fees.

## How much should you save each month?

Use:

**Monthly rent savings = (Target − Amount already saved) ÷ Months until due date.**

If you are starting from zero with 12 months to save ₦780,000, the calculation is:

**₦780,000 ÷ 12 = ₦65,000 per month.**

If you have ₦180,000 already saved, your remaining target becomes ₦600,000. Across 12 months, that is **₦50,000 per month**.

If the full ₦780,000 is due in just nine months, with nothing saved, you need about **₦86,667 every month**. Rounding up slightly protects you from a small shortfall.

| Time left | Monthly savings needed to reach ₦780,000 from zero |
| --- | ---: |
| 12 months | ₦65,000 |
| 9 months | About ₦86,667 |
| 6 months | ₦130,000 |
| 3 months | ₦260,000 |

This table reveals the real problem early. If you take home ₦150,000 and rent demands ₦130,000 monthly savings, you cannot solve the gap through “discipline” alone. You may need a different housing plan, a negotiated arrangement if available, additional legitimate income or a change in timing.

## How much should weekly earners save?

For a full 52-week saving period:

**Weekly contribution = Annual target ÷ 52.**

For ₦780,000, that is **₦15,000 each week**.

If you work only 40 earning weeks, the required contribution becomes **₦19,500 per paid week**. Use the number of weeks when you actually expect income, not 52 if your work is seasonal.

If you are paid by the day, refer to [our daily and weekly budgeting system](/how-to-budget-when-paid-daily-or-weekly-in-nigeria/) to assign a portion of each *net* payment to the rent fund.

## A better system for rent money

### Give rent its own name

Use a clearly labelled savings account, wallet bucket or ledger line called **Next Rent**. Seeing the amount separately makes it harder to confuse it with money for groceries, work or social spending.

Check whether the savings product has minimum balances, withdrawal restrictions, penalties or loss-of-access risks. Do not lock the entire rent payment into an instrument that matures *after* your due date.

### Move the contribution on payday

If your salary arrives on the 26th, set aside the rent contribution on the 26th or as soon as the money clears. Do not wait for “whatever is left” at the end of the month.

If your income varies, move a conservative minimum during slow weeks and add more during good weeks. Our [guide to managing irregular income](/how-to-manage-irregular-income-in-nigeria/) shows how to avoid depending on your best month.

### Track progress in one row

| Date | Contribution | Total saved | Target remaining |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Month 1 | ₦65,000 | ₦65,000 | ₦715,000 |
| Month 2 | ₦65,000 | ₦130,000 | ₦650,000 |
| Month 3 | ₦65,000 | ₦195,000 | ₦585,000 |

Adjust the remaining monthly contribution if you skip or exceed a month.

## What if inflation makes the rent go up?

Avoid promising yourself a renewal figure that your landlord has not confirmed. As the date approaches, discuss the terms early and seek the amount in writing.

You can model two outcomes—for example, **₦720,000 rent** versus **₦780,000**—and see whether your monthly reserve could cover both. These are scenarios, not a forecast of rent inflation.

Do not treat a landlord's proposed increase as automatically lawful or unlawful; tenancy rules and enforceable contract terms vary. If there is a dispute, seek competent local advice.

## Should you borrow to pay house rent?

A rent loan can look like an easy way to settle a large bill, but it changes one predictable obligation into rent **plus interest, fees and loan repayments**.

Before borrowing:

1. Write down exactly what will enter your account after fees.
2. Calculate every repayment and the total paid over the term.
3. Decide how you will start funding the *next* year's rent at the same time.
4. Consider whether a safer housing or payment arrangement is available.

Use our [flat-rate versus reducing-balance loan guide](/flat-rate-vs-reducing-balance-loan-interest-in-nigeria/) and [loan interest calculator](/tools/) for a clearer picture of costs.

Borrowing to pay annual rent can become a cycle if next year's rent reserve does not start immediately.

## Rent savings versus your emergency fund

A rent fund is for a **known bill with a known deadline**. An [emergency fund](/emergency-fund-nigeria/) is for unexpected financial shocks.

Keep them separate in your records, even if the money sits at one bank. If you use rent savings for an emergency, immediately recalculate the remaining monthly contribution. Otherwise the emergency silently becomes another crisis at renewal.

## Common mistakes

- Saving from salary *after* spending the rest.
- Treating rent savings as spare money for social outings.
- Assuming next year's rent will definitely stay the same.
- Ignoring a service charge or moving cost you actually owe.
- Locking funds beyond the payment deadline.
- Borrowing at a high cost without planning the next renewal.
- Using a savings app without checking when withdrawals are possible.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should I save monthly for ₦600,000 annual rent?

Starting from zero over 12 months, **₦50,000 monthly**. If you have fewer months remaining or need additional legitimate expenses, increase the contribution accordingly.

### Is paying annual rent compulsory everywhere in Nigeria?

Payment arrangements and tenancy rights depend on location and contract. This article describes how to *budget for a lump-sum rent payment*, not what a landlord is legally entitled to demand.

### Can I invest rent savings in stocks or cryptocurrency?

Money needed for a near-term fixed bill generally benefits from predictable access and lower volatility. A sharp loss just before renewal could create an avoidable crisis.

### What if I cannot afford the monthly amount?

Recognise the gap as early as possible. Review housing costs, realistic income options and legitimate payment arrangements. Do not hide the shortfall with unrealistic category totals.

## The bottom line

For annual rent, the most useful number is not the final bill. It is the amount you need to set aside **every time you are paid**, calculated from the due date and what you already have.

Start now, keep the reserve visible and review it before the next renewal sneaks up on you.
