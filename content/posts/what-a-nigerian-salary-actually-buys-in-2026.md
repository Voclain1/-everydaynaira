It is payday. Your salary lands, and for about ten minutes you feel rich. Then you pay for transport, buy data, restock the kitchen, send something home, and the money starts to look much smaller than it did on the alert. That feeling is not a failure of discipline. It is arithmetic.

A salary is only as good as what it can buy after the non-negotiables are paid. This guide takes four salaries — ₦70,000, ₦100,000, ₦200,000 and ₦300,000 a month — and shows what each one can realistically cover. Every figure below is a **worked planning scenario, not a national price survey**. Your household size, city and commute will change every amount; the shape of the problem will not. As income rises, the question moves from "how do I survive this month?" to "how do I stop January from undoing December?"

## The gap, in one paragraph

The national minimum wage is **₦70,000 a month**, set by the National Minimum Wage Act 2024. Lagos State pays a higher minimum of ₦85,000. Compare that with one published model: The Guardian's analysis of a single urban worker estimated a basic, cost-reflective lifestyle at about **₦372,300 a month** — roughly ₦125,000 for housing, ₦99,000 for transport (22 working days at about ₦4,500 a day), and ₦47,670 for food, based on the National Bureau of Statistics' healthy-meal cost of ₦1,589 a day. That is one model, with its own assumptions, not an official benchmark. But the gap is real: **₦70,000 against ₦372,300 is a shortfall of more than ₦300,000 a month**, and you live it every time rent, transport and food fall due in the same week.

## Prices are rising more slowly — but food is still the problem

Headline inflation has been falling: 15.39% in August 2026, 14.63% in September, and 14.06% in October. Falling inflation does not mean prices are falling — only that they are rising more slowly. Your market basket still costs more than it did last year.

Food is the exception that matters most. Food inflation was 19.59% in September 2026, about five points above headline inflation. Food is the biggest flexible cost in most households and the hardest to postpone, so when it outruns everything else, it is food that breaks the budget first. Each example below uses the same six lines — housing, food, transport, power and data, other essentials, and savings — so you can see what changes as income rises, and what does not.

## Example 1: ₦70,000 a month — survival first

At the minimum wage there is no room for a perfect budget — only a survival order: food, transport to keep earning, a roof, and a small buffer so one bad week does not become a loan.

| Spending category | Amount | Why it is included |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Housing/rent contribution | ₦10,000 | Family housing or rent reserve |
| Food and groceries | ₦25,000 | Home cooking, market food, staples |
| Transport | ₦15,000 | Commuting to work |
| Power, data and airtime | ₦7,000 | Electricity, phone, internet |
| Other essentials | ₦8,000 | Health, personal care, household |
| Savings/buffer | ₦5,000 | One protected amount |
| **Total** | **₦70,000** | **Nothing unassigned** |

₦25,000 for food is about ₦830 a day — far below the ₦1,589 a day the NBS uses for a healthy meal. That does not mean the table is wrong; it means ₦70,000 cannot buy a full healthy-meal standard for one adult, and most people on this income already know it. The ₦5,000 buffer matters more than it looks: one unexpected expense — a drug, a repair, a fare increase — is what pushes people into borrowing. Protecting it is about keeping one bad week from becoming debt.

## Example 2: ₦100,000 a month — a buffer, at last

An extra ₦30,000 changes the shape of the month: food gets closer to adequate, transport stops being a daily negotiation, and the buffer becomes big enough to absorb something.

| Spending category | Amount | Why it is included |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Housing/rent contribution | ₦20,000 | Rent contribution or reserve |
| Food and groceries | ₦32,000 | Better food cover, home cooking first |
| Transport | ₦20,000 | Regular commute, some margin |
| Power, data and airtime | ₦10,000 | Electricity, data, airtime |
| Other essentials | ₦10,000 | Health, personal care, household |
| Savings/buffer | ₦8,000 | A real buffer |
| **Total** | **₦100,000** | **Nothing unassigned** |

At ₦100,000 you can start planning beyond the month: the ₦8,000 buffer can become an emergency fund, and the housing line a genuine rent reserve. But there is still no room for a large annual rent payment, a school fee or a serious medical bill without rearranging everything else. ₦100,000 is breathing room, not comfort — and the habit that protects it is a weekly check of what you actually spent by Friday.

## Example 3: ₦200,000 a month — the rent reserve changes everything

At ₦200,000, the biggest change is rent: for the first time, you can set aside a serious monthly amount toward an annual payment instead of facing the whole sum in one painful month.

| Spending category | Amount | Why it is included |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Housing/rent reserve | ₦50,000 | Annual rent reserve |
| Food and groceries | ₦55,000 | Adequate food, with margin |
| Transport | ₦35,000 | Commute plus occasional trips |
| Power, data and airtime | ₦15,000 | Electricity, data, subscriptions |
| Other essentials | ₦20,000 | Health, family, household needs |
| Savings/buffer | ₦25,000 | Savings, emergency fund |
| **Total** | **₦200,000** | **Nothing unassigned** |

That ₦50,000 housing line, saved every month, is ₦600,000 in a year. This is the single most important idea in this guide: **rent paid once a year still belongs in a monthly budget**. Treat it as a monthly bill and January stops being a crisis. The ₦25,000 savings line is real now too — 12.5% of income, every month, without starving the food line. Our guide to [saving for annual house rent](/how-to-save-for-annual-house-rent-in-nigeria/) shows how to build the reserve, the [emergency fund guide](/emergency-fund-nigeria/) covers what comes next, and our [₦150,000–₦250,000 salary budget](/how-to-budget-150000-to-250000-naira-salary-in-nigeria/) fits if you sit between those two levels.

## Example 4: ₦300,000 a month — comfort, with conditions

₦300,000 looks comfortable next to the minimum wage, and in many ways it is — but it is also where lifestyle quietly expands to fill the income, and the budget only works if the savings line is protected first.

| Spending category | Amount | Why it is included |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Housing/rent reserve | ₦75,000 | Annual rent reserve |
| Food and groceries | ₦75,000 | Good food cover |
| Transport | ₦45,000 | Commute, fuel or ride-hailing |
| Power, data and airtime | ₦20,000 | Electricity, data, subscriptions |
| Other essentials | ₦30,000 | Health, family, household needs |
| Savings/buffer | ₦55,000 | Savings, emergency fund |
| **Total** | **₦300,000** | **Nothing unassigned** |

The ₦75,000 housing reserve is ₦900,000 a year, and the ₦55,000 savings line is more than 18% of income. Those two lines are the whole point of earning ₦300,000: they buy future calm, not just present comfort. The danger is slow drift — a bigger apartment, a car, subscriptions and social spending that absorb the savings line before the month ends. Move the savings on payday, before spending starts. And if your commute is long, check your real route costs with our [Lagos transport budgeting guide](/how-to-budget-transport-costs-in-lagos-on-a-salary/) instead of guessing.

## What to do at each level

**At ₦70,000:** survival first. Protect food and transport, keep the ₦5,000 buffer untouched unless it is a genuine emergency, and share housing costs wherever you can.

**At ₦100,000:** build the buffer into an emergency fund — ₦8,000 a month is ₦96,000 in a year — and start the rent reserve as a separate line, even if small.

**At ₦200,000:** make the rent reserve automatic and the savings line non-negotiable — a 10% leak here is ₦20,000 a month. Use our [step-by-step monthly budgeting guide](/how-to-create-a-monthly-budget-in-nigeria/) to set the categories up once, then check them weekly.

**At ₦300,000:** pay yourself first, then live on the rest. Move savings on payday, cap the lifestyle lines, and let any pay rise go mostly to savings and the rent reserve.

## Why averages mislead

You will see articles quoting an "average Nigerian salary" far higher than anything you or your friends earn. Ignore them: averages are pulled upward by a small number of very high earners, so the average can be true as a statistic and still describe almost nobody you know.

The same problem applies to percentage rules. The famous 50/30/20 split assumes prices are stable and rent is modest. In Nigeria, rent and food alone can take 60% or more of a modest salary — that does not make you bad at budgeting; it makes the rule wrong for your numbers. Our guide to the [50/30/20 rule for Nigerians](/5030-20-budget-rule-for-nigerians/) explains how to adapt it instead. And the honest way to read any salary article, including this one, is to ask: whose household, which city, what commute? If the answers do not match yours, take the structure and change the amounts.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is ₦70,000 enough to live on in Nigeria in 2026?

For a single person sharing housing and cooking at home, it can cover the basics, but only just — it cannot buy the NBS healthy-meal standard of about ₦1,589 a day. For a family, or anyone paying full rent alone, it is not enough.

### What salary do you need to live comfortably in Lagos in 2026?

A single person needs around ₦200,000 a month for a budget with a real rent reserve and savings, and around ₦300,000 before the month feels comfortable rather than merely covered.

### Why does my salary feel smaller even though inflation is falling?

Because falling inflation means prices rise more slowly, not fall — and food inflation, at 19.59% in September 2026, still outruns overall inflation at 14.63%.

### How much should I save from my salary in Nigeria?

Save something at every level, even ₦5,000 a month at ₦70,000. At ₦200,000, ₦25,000 a month is realistic, and at ₦300,000, protecting ₦55,000 on payday is the move that turns a good salary into security. The amount matters less than the habit of moving it first.

### Should I use the 50/30/20 rule on a Nigerian salary?

Only as a starting point, and only if your essentials fit inside 50%. For most modest salaries, rent and food alone exceed that.

## Bottom line

A salary is not a number. It is a set of choices, made in a particular city, for a particular household, at particular prices. ₦70,000 demands survival discipline. ₦100,000 buys a buffer. ₦200,000 buys a rent reserve. ₦300,000 buys the chance to save seriously — if you protect the savings line first.

Start where you are: write down your real take-home pay, build your own version of the table, and check it weekly.
