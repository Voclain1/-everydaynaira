Rent in Nigeria is usually paid once a year, in advance. Your landlord does not ask for small money every month. He asks for the full year at once.

But your income does not come like that. If you are a market trader, a driver, a freelancer or a POS agent, some weeks are good and some weeks are slow. One week you make plenty. The next week, rain falls, customers stay home, and the money is small.

So how do you save for a big yearly rent when your income changes every week? You do not wait for a good month. You build a plan on your slowest month, and you let the good months help you finish faster.

## Why rent is hard on irregular income

Rent is the biggest bill most Nigerians pay. A salary earner gets the same money every month, so the same saving can leave every month. Your income goes up and down. If you plan with your best month in mind, the plan will break in a slow month. And when the plan breaks, most people stop saving completely. Then rent day arrives, and the money is not there.

The fix is simple. Plan with your worst month, not your best month. If the plan works in a slow month, it will work in every month.

## Step 1: Find your slow-month baseline

Your baseline is the lowest income you can count on in a bad month. Not your average. Not your best month. Your worst normal month.

Look at the last six months. Write down what you earned each month. Find the lowest one. That is your baseline.

For example, a trader in Mile 12 market might earn like this over six months: ₦180,000, ₦150,000, ₦120,000, ₦200,000, ₦140,000 and ₦160,000. Her lowest month is ₦120,000. That is her baseline. She can count on ₦120,000 even when business is slow. Your number will be different. What matters is that it is real. It is money you actually earned in a slow month, not money you hope to earn.

Do not use a month where something strange happened, like a week when you were sick and did not work at all. Use a normal slow month. That is your baseline. If you have not been writing your income down, start now — even two or three months of records are enough. Our guide to [how to manage irregular income in Nigeria](/how-to-manage-irregular-income-in-nigeria/) shows a simple way to track what comes in each week.

## Step 2: Save a fixed amount from the baseline first

Now decide how much to save from that baseline every month. Pick a number you can pay even in a slow month. A good starting point is one third of your baseline. If your baseline is ₦120,000 a month, one third is ₦40,000.

The key word is **first**. When money comes in, move the rent saving out before you spend on anything else. Do not save what is left after spending. There is never anything left after spending. Save first, then spend what remains.

Here is how the trader from our example does it. Her rent is ₦600,000 a year. Her baseline is ₦120,000 a month. She saves ₦40,000 every month, first thing.

In 12 months, that fixed saving alone gives her ₦480,000. That is 12 × ₦40,000 = ₦480,000. It is not the full rent yet. She is short by ₦120,000. That is where the good weeks come in.

## Step 3: Top up from good weeks

Good weeks are your extra push. When you earn more than your baseline, take half of the extra money and add it to the rent saving. The other half is yours to use.

Our trader earns ₦120,000 in a slow month. In a good month she earns ₦140,000. The extra is ₦20,000. Half of that is ₦10,000. She adds ₦10,000 to her rent saving that month.

On average, she adds about ₦10,000 extra every month. Over 12 months, that is 12 × ₦10,000 = ₦120,000.

Now add it up. Fixed saving: ₦480,000. Top-ups: ₦120,000. Total: ₦600,000. That is exactly her rent. The plan works, and it works because the fixed part alone covers most of the rent, while the good weeks only finish the job.

All the numbers above are illustrative. Your rent, your baseline and your saving will be different. But the method is the same: fixed saving from the baseline, plus top-ups from good weeks.

## Step 4: Keep the rent money separate

This step is where many people fail. The money is saved, but it sits in the same account as daily money. Then a small need comes — school fees, a broken phone, a family burial — and the rent money goes. By rent day, it is gone.

Keep the rent money in a separate place. Open a different savings account just for rent, and do not carry its card around. Or use a locked savings plan that does not allow withdrawal until a date you choose. If you prefer cash, use a sealed envelope or a locked box that you do not open. The method does not matter. What matters is that the rent money is not easy to touch.

If your rent money earns small interest while it waits, that is good. And remember the rule: rent money is not emergency money. Build a separate emergency fund — our guide to [how to build an emergency fund with irregular income in Nigeria](/how-to-build-an-emergency-fund-with-irregular-income-in-nigeria/) shows how — so one emergency does not eat your rent.

## Step 5: Know where you stand with the months-remaining formula

A plan you cannot measure is a plan you will abandon. Every month, check where you stand with one simple formula:

**(Rent target − saved so far) ÷ monthly saving = months left**

Let us use our trader again. Her rent target is ₦600,000. After 6 months, she has saved ₦300,000. Her monthly saving is ₦50,000 — that is ₦40,000 fixed plus ₦10,000 average top-up.

Months left = (₦600,000 − ₦300,000) ÷ ₦50,000 = ₦300,000 ÷ ₦50,000 = 6 months.

She has 6 months of rent money left to save, and 6 months before rent is due. She is exactly on track.

If the answer is bigger than the months you have left, you have a problem you can see early. If she had only saved ₦200,000 after 6 months, the formula says (₦600,000 − ₦200,000) ÷ ₦50,000 = 8 months left, but she only has 6. She is 2 months behind. She can fix it now — save a little more each month — instead of finding out on rent day.

## What if the landlord increases the rent?

It happens. You planned for ₦600,000, and the landlord says it is now ₦720,000. Do not panic, and do not throw the plan away.

First, find the new gap. If you have saved ₦400,000 and the new rent is ₦720,000, the gap is ₦720,000 − ₦400,000 = ₦320,000. Divide the gap by the months you have left. If you have 4 months left, you need ₦320,000 ÷ 4 = ₦80,000 a month. If you cannot reach that, talk to your landlord early. Many landlords will accept two payments if you ask early — part now, part in a month or two. Then start the next year with the new rent as your target.

## What not to do

Do not borrow to pay rent. A loan to pay rent only moves the problem to next month, with interest on top. If you are already in a loan cycle, read our guide on [how to break a loan-app rollover cycle in Nigeria](/how-to-break-a-loan-app-rollover-cycle-in-nigeria/) before you borrow again.

Do not wait until three months before rent day to start saving. Saving ₦600,000 in 3 months means ₦200,000 a month. Saving the same amount in 12 months means ₦50,000 a month. Starting early is what makes it possible.

Do not save only when you feel like it. Feelings change with the week. A fixed amount, moved first, does not depend on feelings. And do not keep the rent money where you can spend it without thinking. If it is easy to reach, it will be spent. Make it hard to reach.

## Your plan in five lines

Write this on a page and stick it where you can see it:

1. My rent is ₦______ a year, due in ______ (month).
2. My slow-month baseline is ₦______ a month.
3. I will save ₦______ every month, first, before spending.
4. I will add half of any extra money I earn above my baseline.
5. I will check my months-remaining number on the ______ of every month.

Fill in the blanks with your own numbers. That page is your plan. Follow it for 12 months, and rent day will stop being a fear.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should I save each month for rent?

Start with your slow-month baseline, then save about one third of it every month. If your baseline is ₦120,000, save ₦40,000 a month. Then add top-ups from good months. If one third is too much in a slow month, start with one quarter and increase it when you can. A smaller amount you actually save every month is better than a big amount you only save sometimes.

### What if I earn daily, not monthly?

Use a weekly baseline instead. Find your slowest normal week — for example, ₦25,000 a week. Save a fixed amount from that week, for example ₦7,500 every week. In a year, that is 52 × ₦7,500 = ₦390,000. Then add top-ups from good weeks. The method is the same, only the timing changes.

### What if I miss a month of saving?

Do not stop. Missing one month does not spoil the plan. Use the months-remaining formula to see the new number, and add a small extra amount over the next months to catch up. If you missed ₦40,000 in one month and you have 8 months left, add ₦5,000 extra each month. That is 8 × ₦5,000 = ₦40,000. You are back on track.

### Should I save rent money in a bank or keep it at home?

A bank is safer. Cash at home can be stolen, lost in a fire or flood, or spent on a sudden need. A separate savings account keeps the money safe and out of reach. If you use cash, use a locked box and keep the key with someone you trust, not in your own pocket.

### My rent is due in 3 months and I have saved nothing. What do I do?

First, do not borrow the full rent from a loan app. Second, save as much as you can right now, every week, and talk to your landlord early. Ask to pay in two parts — part now, part in one or two months. Many landlords will agree if you ask early and keep your word. Third, start the 12-month plan the day after you pay, so next year is different.

## The bottom line

Rent is a once-a-year bill, but your income comes in small pieces. The way to join them is simple: find your slow-month baseline, save a fixed amount from it first, top up from good weeks, keep the money separate, and check your months-remaining number every month.

You do not need a big salary. You need a plan that survives a slow month. Start this month, even if the first saving is small. Small small, it adds up — and by rent day, it will be ready.
