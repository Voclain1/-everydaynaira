You spend ₦1,500 getting to work and another ₦1,500 coming home. It does not feel like an enormous amount on Monday. By the end of a 22-day working month, that routine has cost **₦66,000**—before weekend trips, unexpected fares or days you take a different route.

For many workers, transport is one of the most important budget categories because it is repeated frequently and difficult to avoid.

This guide uses **hypothetical fares to demonstrate the calculation**, not a claim about current bus, ride-hailing or fuel prices anywhere in Lagos. Your route, work hours, location and transport options matter more than a city-wide headline.

## Work out your actual monthly commuting cost

The formula is:

**Monthly work commute = (Fare to work + Fare home) × Workdays.**

Add separately the regular non-work journeys you expect to make.

| Scenario | Daily round trip | Days per month | Monthly commute |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Example A | ₦2,000 | 22 | ₦44,000 |
| Example B | ₦3,000 | 22 | ₦66,000 |
| Example C | ₦4,000 | 22 | ₦88,000 |

None of these rows claims to represent a particular bus route or fare schedule.

If you sometimes pay extra for an early-morning ride, include those occasions rather than averaging them away.

## Add the hidden parts of a trip

A commute can contain several smaller payments: tricycle to the main road, bus to the next junction, another vehicle to the office and an additional ride in the evening.

List each leg and count the full return journey.

For example, if the main bus ride costs ₦1,200 each way but your first and last mile costs ₦500 each way, the actual round trip is:

**(₦1,200 + ₦500) × 2 = ₦3,400.**

At 22 days, that is **₦74,800 per month**. Under-budgeting by ₦1,000 a day leaves a ₦22,000 hole in the month.

Your transport category should also include essential work-related errands that you cannot reasonably avoid.

## Compare transport with take-home pay

If you take home ₦150,000 and commuting costs ₦66,000, commuting consumes **44% of salary**.

That does not mean a transport percentage above a specific threshold breaks a rule; it is evidence that the budget has limited room left for food, housing, power and savings.

Our [₦150,000–₦250,000 salary budget examples](/how-to-budget-150000-to-250000-naira-salary-in-nigeria/) show how to allocate other essential categories, but they cannot make a high commute cost disappear.

Check how much of the salary remains **after commuting and known contractual obligations**. If necessary expenses exceed income, you need a structural change or additional income, not a fake percentage allocation.

## Can a different route save money?

Compare an alternative route on four factors:

| Factor | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Total fare | Include every stage, not just the main bus |
| Reliability | A late arrival can cause work problems |
| Safety | An unsafe shortcut is not a reasonable saving |
| Travel time | Extra hours affect rest, childcare and work |
| Flexibility | Late closing or rainy days may change the return fare |

Sometimes using a more expensive reliable option on a difficult day is justified. The goal is a sustainable plan, not the lowest possible payment at any personal cost.

If you own a vehicle, count fuel, maintenance, insurance, parking and financing costs rather than comparing only petrol with bus fare.

## What if your employer allows some remote days?

Calculate the real change in travel days.

If your daily round trip is ₦3,000 and you commute 22 days, the month costs ₦66,000. If you work remotely on four of those days, the corresponding commuting cost becomes **₦54,000**, a potential saving of **₦12,000**, before any added home-power or data expenses.

Don't assume remote work always saves money without considering connectivity and electricity costs.

## Use a weekly commute envelope

If you need ₦15,000 to commute on five workdays, allocate that amount at the beginning of the week instead of taking random withdrawals each morning.

Track actual fares for two weeks and adjust your plan. Do not depend on an old fare after a route, fuel or transport policy changes.

If your pay is daily or weekly, use [the irregular-pay budgeting guide](/how-to-budget-when-paid-daily-or-weekly-in-nigeria/) so transport money is protected before discretionary spending.

## Longer-term options deserve careful maths

Moving closer to work may reduce commute costs but increase rent and moving expenses. Before deciding:

**Annual net change = Annual transport savings − Additional annual housing and moving costs.**

That calculation does not capture every personal benefit or drawback, but it prevents a move that appears cheaper only because rent wasn't included.

If your job is uncertain, avoid locking yourself into a high fixed housing cost on the assumption that commuting savings will always be available.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should I budget for transport in Lagos?

Use your real route fare, journey count and practical buffer. There is no single reliable city-wide budget that fits everyone.

### Should I include weekend outings?

Yes, but separate essential family trips from discretionary outings so you can adjust them when money is tight.

### Do ride-hailing promotions count as savings?

Only when the discount is actually available. Do not plan every commute around an uncertain promotional price.

### Is buying a car always cheaper?

No. Consider ownership, insurance, fuel, maintenance, parking and financing. Compare the whole cost, not one day's petrol.

## The bottom line

**Multiply your real round-trip fare by the days you travel.** That one calculation makes the transport budget visible. Then test any change against the full household budget, safety and the demands of your work.
