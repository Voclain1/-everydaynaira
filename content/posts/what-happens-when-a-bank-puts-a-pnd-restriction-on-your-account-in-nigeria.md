You wake up on a Monday morning. Rent is due, so you open your bank app to send the money. The transfer fails. You try again. It fails again. You try the ATM, and the card does not work either. Your balance is still there on the screen, but your money will not move.

That is what a bank restriction feels like. Nobody called to explain. This guide explains it in plain words: what a PND is, why it happens, and the steps to get your account working again.

## What is a PND?

PND means Post No Debit. It is a bank instruction that stops money from leaving an account. "Debit" is just the bank's word for money going out. So a Post No Debit means: no money goes out.

While a PND is on your account, you usually cannot send a transfer, withdraw cash, pay with your card, or pay a bill from that account. But in most cases, money can still come in. Your salary can still land. A friend can still send you money. You can still open the app and see your balance. That is the main difference between a PND and a full account freeze.

There is also a stricter version, Post No Debit or Credit, where money cannot come in either — a transfer sent to you may fail or be sent back. So always ask your bank first: "Is it PND only, or PND or Credit?" If your salary is due this week, the answer matters.

## Why would a bank do this?

A bank does not place a PND to punish you. It places one because a rule, a report or an instruction tells it to. Here are the common reasons.

**1. Your BVN or NIN is missing, or the details do not match.** This is the most common reason today. In December 2023, the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) directed that bank accounts and wallets must have a BVN or NIN linked to them. Tier 1 accounts need a BVN or a NIN. Tier 2 and Tier 3 accounts need both. From 1 March 2024, funded accounts without them were to be placed on Post No Debit or Credit. So if you opened an account years ago with only a phone number and never linked your BVN or NIN, that may be the reason. A mismatch can cause the same problem — your BVN says "Adaeze Ngozi Okafor" and your NIN says "Adaeze Okafor" — and this kind is usually the easiest to fix: link the missing number or correct the wrong record. If your BVN or NIN itself has been stolen or misused, read our guide on [what to do if your BVN or NIN has been compromised](/what-to-do-if-your-bvn-or-nin-has-been-compromised-in-nigeria/).

**2. A fraud report or suspicious activity.** If someone reports your account for fraud, or the bank's system flags a strange transaction, the bank may restrict the account while it checks. For example, an account that has never received more than ₦100,000 in five years suddenly receives ₦1,000,000 from a stranger. The bank may want to know where that money came from before it lets it move again. The same can happen when someone reports that money was sent to your account by mistake, or that your account was used in a scam. You may not be the one who did anything wrong. The bank is still allowed to check first. A 2024 Court of Appeal case, involving Kuda Microfinance Bank, found that a bank may lawfully restrict an account without a court order first where there is a fraud report or suspicious activity and the customer agreed to such measures in the account terms — which most customers do when they open an account. If money left your account without your permission, our guide on [what to do after an unauthorised debit](/what-to-do-after-an-unauthorized-debit-in-nigeria/) covers the first steps.

**3. A court order.** A court can order a bank to stop money from leaving an account — often in a debt case, a divorce case, or a dispute about money. Only the court process can lift it. Your bank cannot simply remove it because you asked. You will need to deal with the case itself, and that is a time to speak to a lawyer.

**4. An instruction from the police, the EFCC or another authority.** Law-enforcement agencies can ask a bank to restrict an account during an investigation. The bank may not be able to share all the details, but it should be able to tell you that an instruction exists and which authority gave it.

**5. An unsettled dispute or a documentation problem.** Sometimes the reason is smaller: the bank needs a document from you, or a letter went to an old address. Check your email, including spam, and your app notifications first. And know this: only your own bank can tell you the exact reason and only your own bank can lift it. Not another bank, not an agent, not anyone who calls offering help for a fee.

## What you can and cannot do while it lasts

A restriction does not mean your money is gone. Your balance is still yours. The bank has only stopped it from moving, for now.

You can usually still see your balance and statement, receive money into the account (if it is PND only), and speak to the bank about the restriction. You usually cannot send money out in any way, withdraw cash, or use the card linked to that account.

So what about the bills that cannot wait? Do not panic and do not borrow at a high cost to cover a short problem. Use another account if you have one. If the restriction is PND or Credit, ask your employer to hold your salary or send it to a different account until the restriction is lifted, and tell anyone who owes you money to wait — a payment that bounces back is easier to fix than one stuck in a restricted account.

## How to get it lifted: six steps

Do these in order. Write everything down as you go.

**Step 1: Find out the exact reason.** Go to the branch in person — a restriction is rarely fixed by a chatbot. Ask two questions and write down the answers: "What is the exact reason?" and "Is it PND only, or PND or Credit?" Also ask when it was placed and who placed it: the bank, a court, or an authority. If the bank says it cannot tell you everything, ask what it can tell you, and why.

**Step 2: Ask what the bank needs from you, in writing.** It may be your BVN and NIN, a valid ID, proof of address, or documents about one transaction — for example, what a large payment was for and who sent it. If one transfer seems to have caused the problem, note its amount, date and reference; our guide on [how to find your NIP session ID](/how-to-find-nip-session-id-for-bank-transfer-in-nigeria/) shows where that lives. Get the name of the person you spoke to and a reference number for your case.

**Step 3: Provide the documents.** Bring what was asked for, through the bank's official channel — at the branch or through the app, not a WhatsApp number someone gave you. If the problem is a missing BVN or NIN, link it in the app or at the branch. You can check your BVN by dialling *565*0# from your registered line. If the two numbers do not match, correct the wrong record first. Keep a copy of everything you submit.

**Step 4: Ask for a date and a written answer.** Ask when the restriction will be reviewed and when you will hear back. Then ask for the decision in writing: either the restriction is lifted, or the bank explains why it is not. Do not leave with only a verbal promise. A written answer is your evidence if you need to complain later.

**Step 5: Test with a small transfer.** Once the bank says the restriction is lifted, do not send your rent first. Send a small amount — an illustrative ₦1,000 to your own other account or to a trusted person — and confirm it goes through. Then check your balance again. If the small transfer works, your account is back.

**Step 6: If the bank will not explain or act, complain in writing.** Complain to your bank's complaints unit first, in writing, through a channel that gives you proof — an email you keep, or a letter with a stamped copy. State what happened, when it started, what you have done, and what you want. The CBN's guidance generally expects you to give your bank about two weeks before you escalate. Keep the acknowledgement and tracking number the bank gives you.

If the bank does not resolve it, escalate to the CBN Consumer Protection Department. The CBN publishes the email cpd@cbn.gov.ng for complaints, and it has a complaints portal as well. Our guide to [how to report a bank to the CBN in Nigeria](/how-to-report-a-bank-to-cbn-in-nigeria/) walks through the full process, including what documents to attach. Do not include your PIN, password, card details or one-time password (OTP) in any complaint. The CBN itself warns customers never to share those.

One honest warning: a complaint does not cancel a court order or a law-enforcement instruction. If a court or an authority is behind the restriction, the bank may not be able to lift it just because you complained.

## What NOT to do

- **Do not pay anyone who promises to unfreeze your account.** There is no official agent who can lift a bank restriction for a fee. Anyone who contacts you offering this is almost certainly trying to steal from you. Only your bank can lift it.
- **Do not share your OTP, PIN, password or BVN with anyone** who calls or messages you about the restriction. A real bank officer will never ask for these. If someone asks, end the call.
- **Do not open a new account to dodge a court order.** If a court has restricted your account, opening another one to move the same money does not solve the case. It can make your trouble bigger.
- **Do not ignore the bank's letters and calls.** The restriction will not go away on its own. The sooner you find out the reason, the sooner you can fix it.

Think of Ngozi, a teacher in Enugu. She opened her account in 2019 with only her phone number and never linked her NIN. One Monday, her transfer to her landlord failed — much like a [bank transfer that fails but still debits you](/bank-transfer-failed-but-debited-in-nigeria/), except here nothing moved at all. At the branch she learned her account was on Post No Debit or Credit for the missing NIN. She linked it, got written confirmation, and the next day a small test transfer of ₦1,000 to her sister went through. The whole wahala lasted two days — because she found out the reason first, instead of guessing.

Your case may take longer, especially if a fraud report or court order is involved. But the first step is always the same: find out the exact reason, in writing, from your own bank.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a PND the same as my account being closed?

No. A PND stops money from leaving, but the account is still open and the balance is still yours. A closed account is different — the bank has ended it entirely. If your bank says your account is closed, ask for that in writing, and ask what happens to the money in it.

### Can money still come into my account during a PND?

Usually yes, if it is a PND only. Money coming in is called a credit, and a PND blocks debits, not credits. But if the restriction is Post No Debit or Credit, money cannot come in either. Ask your bank which one is on your account, because the answer matters for your salary and for anyone sending you money.

### How long does it take to lift a PND?

It depends on the reason. A missing BVN or NIN can be fixed in a day or two. A fraud investigation or a court order can take weeks or longer. There is no single deadline for every case, so ask your bank for a date and get it in writing.

### Can my bank place a PND without telling me?

Guides that have read the CBN's BVN framework report that, where a restriction follows a reported breach, the bank should notify the customer through a verifiable means within five business days and give the customer a chance to present documents. We could not verify that five-day rule from the CBN's own framework text, so treat it as reported, not confirmed — and check your SMS, email and app notifications first, including spam, because a message may already be waiting.

### What if the bank says it is a court order but will not show it to me?

Ask the bank to confirm in writing that a court order exists, which court made it, and what it covers. If you believe the order does not exist or has expired, that is a matter for a lawyer — a complaint to the bank alone may not settle it.

## The bottom line

A PND means your money cannot leave your account — for now. It does not mean your money is gone. Find out the exact reason, in writing, from your own bank. Fix what can be fixed: link your BVN and NIN, correct a mismatch, or provide the documents asked for. If the bank will not explain or act, complain in writing, then escalate to the CBN. Never pay a stranger, or share your OTP or PIN, to get your account back.
