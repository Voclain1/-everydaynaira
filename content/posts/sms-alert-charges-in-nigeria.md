Your bank takes ₦3 here, ₦5 there, and by the end of the month you have a long line of alert charges. Is that allowed? Should every message cost money? Can you turn off SMS without missing fraud warnings?

The answer depends on **why the alert was sent** and the bank's disclosed tariff. The same kind of deduction can be ordinary in one situation and questionable in another.

This article explains the rules and gives you a simple way to count the cost before you change your notification settings.

## What the CBN guide says about SMS alerts

In section 10.10 of its published [2020 Guide to Charges](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2019/CCD/Guide%20to%20Charges%20by%20Banks%20Other%20Financial%20and%20Non-Financial%20Institutions%20eff%20Jan%201%202020.pdf), the Central Bank of Nigeria describes the approach to transaction alerts for banks:

| Type of notification | Published 2020 rule | Practical question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SMS on a customer-induced transaction | Cost recovery | Does the bank's tariff and actual message count support the deduction? |
| SMS on a bank-induced transaction | No charge | Was the message caused by the bank rather than your action? |
| Email notification | No charge | Can you receive reliable email alerts instead? |

**Cost recovery does not mean that the bank can choose an unlimited fee.** It means the charge is linked to the service cost rather than a freely invented surcharge. But the guide does not set one nationwide fixed price per SMS in that section.

The CBN circulated an updated charges document as an **April 2026 exposure draft**. A draft should not be treated as a confirmed operative replacement without checking a final regulatory instruction and your bank's current tariff.

## What counts as a customer-induced transaction?

Examples may include a withdrawal, debit-card payment or transfer you initiated. A notification about an activity generated by the bank itself may be categorised differently.

There are edge cases: a recurring standing instruction, an attempted transfer, a reversal notice, a security alert or multiple messages about one transaction. Rather than assuming every charge is illegal, ask your bank:

- Which underlying transaction caused this message?
- Was it classified as customer-induced or bank-induced?
- What price did the bank apply and where is that price published?
- Was the same transaction charged for more than one alert?

A proper breakdown is more useful than an argument about a number on its own.

## How much can SMS alerts cost per month?

Here is a **hypothetical example**, not a statement of any bank's official tariff:

| Charged SMS messages in one month | If your bank charged ₦5 per SMS |
| --- | --- |
| 10 | ₦50 |
| 30 | ₦150 |
| 60 | ₦300 |
| 100 | ₦500 |

The maths is simple: **number of billable messages × your bank's actual price**. Your real cost could be lower, higher or zero, depending on the institution, product and notification method.

If you receive 60 alerts but your statement contains charges for 100, ask why. It could involve messages that were delivered but not noticed, bundled billing or an error. Your bank should be able to identify the billable activity.

## How to audit your SMS deductions

### Step 1: List the charges

Open your bank statement for one full month and filter for lines marked SMS, notification, alert or similar. Record each charge and its date.

### Step 2: Compare with the messages and underlying transactions

Count your own initiated transfers, card payments or withdrawals for the corresponding days. Do not assume the SMS inbox is a perfect record: messages can be deleted, blocked or sent to a different registered number.

### Step 3: Request the bank's tariff

Use the institution's published schedule or customer service. Ask whether the rate changed, whether taxes apply and whether a notification package exists for your account type.

### Step 4: Challenge the charges you cannot reconcile

If several deductions appear for a single transaction, or a charge was applied to a bank-induced message that should be free under the applicable rule, request a formal investigation.

## Can you disable SMS alerts in Nigeria?

The published 2020 CBN guide treats SMS transaction alerts as mandatory by default and says that where a customer opts out, the customer should issue an **indemnity** concerning potential losses. That is a serious consideration, not a button to tap without thinking.

Some banks may let you choose email, app push notifications or a combination. **Do not turn off every form of alert**, especially if you use your card online or transact frequently.

Before switching:

- Check that your registered email address and app access are secure.
- Turn on notifications for your banking app.
- Confirm that you will still receive timely alerts for transactions you did not authorise.
- Ask your bank whether an SMS opt-out requires paperwork or an indemnity.
- Keep a habit of reviewing your transaction history, not just waiting for notifications.

Free email alerts are only useful if you actually check the mailbox and protect its login.

## When SMS charges can be a warning sign

**Repeated charges without a matching service.** Ask for an itemised explanation instead of assuming the total is harmless.

**A sudden tariff change you did not know about.** Ask for the applicable price list and the bank's notice.

**An SMS fee linked to an unrecognised debit.** First investigate the underlying transaction as a possible security issue. Our [unauthorised-debit checklist](/what-to-do-after-an-unauthorized-debit-in-nigeria/) explains how.

**A support agent asks for OTPs or passwords to cancel alerts.** Stop the conversation and contact the bank through a verified channel. Notification preferences should never require revealing a security secret.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are SMS alerts free in Nigeria?

Not necessarily. The published 2020 CBN guide permits cost-recovery charges for customer-induced SMS alerts and states that bank-induced alerts should be free.

### What is the official CBN SMS alert rate?

That section of the 2020 guide states a **cost-recovery principle**, not one universal fixed naira price. Check the tariff of your particular bank.

### Is email notification free?

The published 2020 guide states **no charge** for email notification. Your bank should explain the options available to your account.

### Can my bank charge for an SMS about a transaction I never made?

First treat an unknown transaction as a potential security problem. Report it promptly, then ask the bank to explain the alert and any associated fees.

### Does this apply to all fintech wallets?

The exact licence, product and applicable regulation matter. Do not assume every wallet has the same tariff as a deposit money bank.

## The bottom line

Small charges deserve the same basic test as large ones: **what service, what rate and what evidence?** Review a month's deductions, compare with the bank's tariff, and switch to secure alternatives only after understanding the trade-offs.

If the bank cannot explain the bill, ask for a formal complaint reference. If it remains unresolved, follow the [CBN consumer complaints process](/how-to-report-a-bank-to-cbn-in-nigeria/).
