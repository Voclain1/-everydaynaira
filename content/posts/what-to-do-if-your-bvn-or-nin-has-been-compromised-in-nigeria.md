Your phone rings. The voice sounds calm and sure. "Good afternoon, I am calling from your bank. Your BVN has expired. If you do not confirm it now, your account will be blocked today."

You read out the number. Then the person asks for the code that just came to your phone. You read that too. The call ends. Then the debit alerts start.

If this sounds familiar, breathe. You can still act. What you do in the next few hours matters most. Follow the steps below, in order.

## What are BVN and NIN, in plain words?

Your BVN is your Bank Verification Number. It is an 11-digit number that ties all your bank accounts together under one identity. The Central Bank of Nigeria set it up, and NIBSS — the Nigeria Inter-Bank Settlement System — keeps the central record. Your fingerprints and your face are linked to it.

Your NIN is your National Identification Number. It is also 11 digits. NIMC, the National Identity Management Commission, gives it to you.

Your BVN alone cannot empty your account. The danger is what a fraudster can do when they combine it with other things: your phone number, your date of birth, your OTP, or your PIN. That combination can open accounts in your name. So the goal now is simple. Cut off the other pieces.

## How does this usually happen?

**The fake bank call.** Someone calls and says they are from your bank, or from the CBN. They say your BVN has expired, or your account will be blocked. Then they ask you to confirm your BVN, your card number, or the OTP on your phone. A real bank will never do this. The CBN has said so clearly: neither the CBN nor any bank will ever call, text or email you to ask for your password, card details or PIN. If a call asks for any of those, na scam. End the call.

**The phishing link.** A text or WhatsApp message says your account has a problem and gives you a link. The page looks like your bank's page. It asks for your BVN, card details and PIN together. No real process asks for all three at once. Do not tap links in such messages. Open your bank's app yourself. The WhatsApp version of this trick is the most common one today — see [how to spot fake job and investment scams on WhatsApp in Nigeria](/how-to-spot-fake-job-and-investment-scams-on-whatsapp-in-nigeria/).

**Careless sharing.** You fill a form online because someone promised a loan, a grant, or a job. The form asks for your BVN and NIN.

**The agent who keeps your details.** At a registration centre or a roadside stand, someone helps you register and writes down your details "for later". Some sell the details. Give your BVN or NIN only inside a bank branch, inside your bank's official app, or to a service you started yourself.

If one of these happened to you, you are not foolish. Most scams in Nigeria use the same few stories — our guide to [online scams in Nigeria](/online-scams-in-nigeria/) lists them.

## Step 1: Tell your bank first — today, not tomorrow

Call your bank on the number in its official app, or the number printed on your card. Do not call the number from the suspicious message. If you can, go to a branch in person.

Say this, plainly: "I think my BVN [or NIN] has been compromised." Say when it happened and what exactly you shared. Ask the bank to check your accounts for any transaction you did not make, to check if any new account has been opened with your BVN, and to place a flag on your profile.

Then write down three things: the name of the person you spoke to, the time of the call, and a reference or ticket number. If you went to a branch, ask for a copy of your written complaint, stamped. Keep it. If you have accounts in more than one bank, tell every bank. Your BVN links them all.

If money has already left your account, tell the bank at once and ask what can be reversed. Our guide to [what to do after an unauthorised debit in Nigeria](/what-to-do-after-an-unauthorized-debit-in-nigeria/) walks through that part. And if the bank is slow to act, you can complain to the CBN — see [how to report a bank to the CBN in Nigeria](/how-to-report-a-bank-to-cbn-in-nigeria/).

## Step 2: Check for accounts or loans opened in your name

A fraudster who has your BVN and NIN may not touch your own account at all. They may open a new one in your name and use it to collect money from other victims. Or they may take a loan in your name. You may only find out months later, when a debt collector calls.

So ask your bank this direct question: "Has any account been opened with my BVN that I did not open?" Your bank can check its own records. It can also ask NIBSS, which keeps the central BVN record for all banks, to help check.

One thing we could not verify, and you should know: we could not find a public website or app where you, as an individual, can type in your BVN and see a list of all the accounts linked to it. Any website that promises this is likely a scam itself. Do not enter your BVN on such a site. The check goes through your bank.

What you can check yourself is your own BVN. Dial *565*0# from the phone number linked to your bank account, and your BVN appears on your screen. Never read the result out to a caller.

## Step 3: Report to the police — and to the NDPC if your data was misused

**Go to the police.** Visit the nearest police station and make a formal report. Take your ID, your bank's reference number, and any evidence you have: screenshots of the messages, the phone number that called you, the link you tapped, the date and time. Ask for a copy of your statement or a police report number. You will need it if a loan or account was opened in your name.

**Report to the EFCC too, if money was stolen.** The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission investigates financial fraud. You can report through its website, efcc.gov.ng, or visit its nearest office.

**Report to the NDPC if your personal data was misused.** The NDPC is the Nigeria Data Protection Commission, the regulator for personal data in Nigeria, set up under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023. Raise it with the organisation first if one misused your data, then complain to the NDPC through the contact details on ndpc.gov.ng. The NDPC also runs a breach reporting portal at services.ndpc.gov.ng/breach, but that portal is mainly for organisations to report a breach. As an individual, your path is a complaint to the NDPC. Do not pay anyone who offers to "report to NDPC" for you for a fee.

Keep copies of everything you submit, and every reference number you are given.

## Step 4: Watch your accounts for three months

Check your bank statements every week, not once a month. Look for small debits you do not know. A small debit of ₦500 or ₦1,000, for example, is often a test: the fraudster is checking whether the account still works. The figures here are illustrative — the point is the pattern, not the amount.

Turn on alerts for every transaction, and change your passwords and PINs. Turn on two-factor authentication wherever your bank or email offers it.

Watch your phone too. If it suddenly loses signal for no clear reason and does not come back, call your network from another phone, then call your bank. If the network does nothing, escalate to the NCC, the Nigerian Communications Commission.

Tell your close contacts as well. One short message is enough: "Someone has my BVN. If anyone calls you about my account, do not share anything. Call me first."

## What NOT to share next

From today, do not share these with anyone who calls or messages you first: your OTP, the one-time code from your bank; your PIN or your password; your full card number, the expiry date, and the three digits at the back of your card; your BVN or NIN, unless you are inside a bank branch or inside your bank's official app, in a process you started yourself.

No real bank, no real government office, and no real regulator will ask for these on a phone call. If a caller says "I need your OTP to unblock your account," that sentence alone tells you it is a scam. End the call and call your bank yourself.

## If a loan or account was already opened in your name

Do not pay the loan. Paying it can look like you accepted it. Instead, write to the lender. State, in writing, that you did not take the loan, that your identity was stolen, and that you have reported the matter to the police and your bank. Attach your police report number and your bank's reference number. Ask the lender to freeze the account and investigate. Keep a copy of your letter.

Ask the lender for the details: when was the account opened, and what phone number and address were used? Report the lender to the FCCPC if it is a loan app, and to the CBN if it is a bank. Our guide to [how to report loan app harassment in Nigeria](/how-to-report-loan-app-harassment-in-nigeria/) explains the complaint steps. Keep every paper — every letter, reply, reference number and screenshot — in one folder, on paper and on your phone, for at least a year.

We could not verify a single hotline or portal that clears a false loan from your name in one step. The real process is slower: report in writing, to the lender, the police, and the regulator, and follow up until you have a written answer.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can someone empty my account with only my BVN?

No. A BVN alone cannot move money out of your account. The danger is when a fraudster combines your BVN with your phone number, your date of birth, your OTP, or your PIN. The same is true of your NIN.

### I already shared my BVN with a caller. Is it too late?

No. Tell your bank today. Ask for a flag on your profile and a check for new accounts. Change your passwords and PIN. Many people who act fast lose nothing.

### Should I report to the police, or is telling my bank enough?

Tell your bank first, then report to the police. The police report creates an official record, and you will need it if a loan or account was opened in your name. If money was stolen, also report to the EFCC.

### A website says it can check whether my BVN is blacklisted. Should I use it?

No. We could not verify any public website where you can check your BVN status or see the accounts linked to it. Such sites are a known fraud risk. Check with your bank directly, in a branch or through its official app.

## The bottom line

If your BVN or NIN has been compromised, do four things, in this order. Tell your bank first. Check for accounts or loans opened in your name. Report to the police, and to the NDPC if your data was misused. Then watch your accounts for three months.

Do not share your OTP, PIN or password with anyone who calls you. Do not pay anyone who promises to fix it for a fee.

Act fast, act in order, and keep every paper. That is how you take your identity back.
